# Findings

**PACEMAKER on rsk: where the source could not be met, and what was awkward on rsk**

A standing document (PM31.2; task 9.7), begun 2026-11-22 at the end of the first build of stage E1 and completed for task 23.2 on 2026-11-25: section D gives the answers that task asks for, and section C what is still not known. Each finding says what was found, where, and what was done about it. "Open" means nothing was decided. Requirement references `PMn.m` point into `requirements.md`, `PD-nn` into `design.md`; `Rn.m`, `PR-nn`, `DD-nn` into rsk's requirements and design.

## A. Where the source's behaviour is not met

| # | Finding | Where | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | **A Sense just before a Pace does not cancel it.** A Sense is acted on by a Job, so one that precedes a pending Pace by less than the sense Task's response time finds the compare already fired: both occur. The source says a Sense cancels the pending Pace. | PD-04, PM5.5, PM-PAR-15 | The requirement states a window in which the Pace need not be cancelled (PM-PAR-15: 2 ms as proposed, 4 ms since the measured response times of task 9.2); the step function records the Pace as a fusion; the judge counts them. Measured on the board (task 9.5, 24 runs of VVI against a rhythm a little faster than the LRL): a Sense 144 µs before the Pace no longer cancelled it, one 220 µs before did. See section D. |
| A2 | **A Pace whose instant cannot be met is late, not dropped.** When the instant asked for is closer than the guard, the Pace is placed one lead (500 µs) past the present. Measured: late by 609 µs at most in the sweep through the Application. | "Arming and the lead" | Within the 8 ms tolerance. A triggered Pace (PM5.4) is by construction one lead after its Sense is served. |
| A3 | **A pulse can be made by software.** If three rounds of arming all fail, the pin is set directly and the pulse's length is software's. | `pm-hw`, `Armed::Late` | Never reached on the board (no forced pulse in any run at a lead of 100 µs or more); reached in the host model only under holds of 700 µs. Counted (`late_arms`) so that a rig run would show it. |
| A4 | **The parameter set is narrower than Table 19-1 for now.** A set naming a Mode outside Off, AOO, VOO, AAI and VVI, or turning hysteresis or rate smoothing on, is refused. | `pm_core::validate`, PM19.1 | On purpose: a set is never accepted to be delivered differently. **No longer so**: widened with the stages, and since task 19.5 `validate` admits the nineteen Modes of Table 19-1 with every parameter. |
| A5 | **No interactive limit applies to a single-chamber Mode.** Under Table 19-4 none uses the URL, so "VRP < URL interval" never applies, and VVI with a VRP of 500 ms at 175 ppm (343 ms) is accepted: every Sense is then a Refractory sense. | Table 19-5, PM19.3 | Open. A limit against the LRL interval would close it; it is a choice of the specification, for the user. |
| A6 | **The pace copies.** Each pace compare drives a second pin so that the Heart_Simulator sees the pulse. A PG whose pace pins reached the heart would not have them. | "Hardware signal flow" | A property of this rig's wiring. That the pin and its copy move at one instant is the PPI's property and was not measured; nothing is judged on the copy. |
| A7 | **A debugger's access restarts the therapy.** It halts the core, the Jobs due meanwhile miss their deadlines, and the declared response restarts the Partition. | PM23.3 | The declared response working. It cost a measurement: a record kept in the Partition cannot be read from a running PG (the sweep has a declaration that only records). |

| A8 | **No electrogram.** | PM18.4 | Finding B46: at a boundary of digital signals it would be the Marker stream again. Agreed by the user, 2026-11-25. |
| A9 | **At ERT and ERP the measured data and the Markers were still supplied; repaired 2026-11-25.** PM17.7 has the PG disable temporary programming, the threshold test, measured data, electrograms and Markers at those levels. Temporary pacing and the threshold test were declined there (tasks 16.5, 22.6) and there is no electrogram; but `diag` does not know the battery level, and `link`, which does, handed every request on. Found when the traceability table was completed (task 23.1), not by a test: no test asked for a record at ERT. | `apps/pacemaker/comms/src/proto.rs` (`level`, `asks_disabled`, `record_in`), PM17.7 | **Closed on the user's word.** `link` answers a read of the measured data, a read of a Marker and MARKERS_START with DECLINED at ERT and below and does not hand them to `diag`; the SET_BATTERY that brings the level there ends a Marker transmission; a record of either kind that still comes is not told and is counted. The level is the worse of the DCM's last and the one in `control`'s status, so a restart of `comms` or of `therapy` does not open it again. A host test over the five levels; the member of `comms` proves 41 files with each pair of solvers; on the rig (`e5_runs.py ert`) declined at ERT and at ERP with no Marker coming, supplied at BOL before and after. Two consequences to know: the battery voltage and the date of the last interrogation are words of the measured data, so at ERT only the battery status level is read (PM17.3 against PM17.7; the level is in every STATUS); and for the 50 to 100 ms after the DCM sets a better level, until `control`'s status says so too, a request is still declined. **A hole in the repair, found the same day when its contract was written and closed:** a record `link` took while the level was good was told although `control`'s status had brought ERT in between (the check was at the taking and at SET_BATTERY only). The check is now where the record is told, and that is the place of the proof: `answer`'s contract states that at ERT or below a RECORD frame is sent only of a record the level does not disable, and that no disabled request is put for `diag`; four mutants (`proofs.md`). |
| A10 | **The clock is not set.** PM20.1 lists "set the clock" among the DCM's commands. SET_CLOCK is answered with a status, which is how the DCM_Stub asks for one, and sets nothing: the PG keeps no calendar, its trend's stamps are the Kernel's milliseconds since the start (PM-ASM-05), and the dates it reports are values the DCM supplied (the implant data, the date of the last interrogation). | `proto.rs`, PM20.1, PM17.3, PM18.3 | Recorded. A clock value kept by `diag` and added to the stamps would meet the wording; nothing would use it but the DCM that supplied it. |
| A11 | **The MSR is not independent of the URL in VDDR and DDDR.** | PM15.2, Table 19-5 | The user's decision of 2026-11-25, so that Property P1 holds of every set the PG accepts (section "A, continued"). |
| A12 | **Programmed parameters, the histograms, the trend and the implant data do not survive a reset**, and a restart of the Partition that holds them loses them too. | PM30.2, PM-NG-06 | Recorded as the requirement asks: rsk's Target gives a Partition no non-volatile memory, and none was added. |
| A13 | **Amplitudes, sensitivities, impedances and voltages are numbers.** The pace pin has two levels and the sense input is an edge; a wave amplitude, a lead impedance and a battery voltage are values the DCM_Stub supplies and reads back, marked as simulated. | PM-NG-03, PM2.4, PM17.1 to PM17.3, PM19.2, PM19.4 | By the experiment's scope. What can be judged of a pulse is its instant and its width, and both are. |

- **In the Kernel's safe state there is no pacing** (PM23.5; recorded as required, task 17.3). At the end of the Operating_Duration, and after any fault whose response is the safe state, rsk stops every Job: no compare is armed again, both pace pins stay low, and nothing paces until the board is reset. The board has no pacing outside the processor and none is simulated. On the rig: 90 Paces in 44.5 s, then none; the pins low; the last pulse whole. A device for use needs back-up pacing that does not depend on the processor (PM-NG-01).

## B. What was awkward on rsk

| # | Finding | Where | What was done |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | **An Application cannot have a library crate with code of its own.** The Link_Checker allows executable symbols from the Kernel crates, `rsk`, the system crate and the Partition crates only (DD-11). `pm-core` and `pm-hw`, which the design shares between the Partition, the host tools and the tests, were linked into the shared code region and refused. | Link_Checker, task 8.4 | Every function of the two crates is `#[inline(always)]`, so their code is instantiated in the Partition that calls it and they have no symbol. It holds for these crates; the Link_Checker says so if one stops inlining. A second Partition that uses `pm-core` (stage E3) gets its own copy, which is what isolation wants. Whether rsk should know library crates is open. |
| B2 | **rsk-entry names every Application.** It binds one system crate per cargo feature, so the Heart_Simulator, the PPI probe and the pulse generator each edited a Kernel crate, and each forwards its own features through it. | `crates/rsk-entry`, tasks 4.2, 3.6, 8.1 | Done three times. It is the only edit of rsk outside Requirement 33, and it contradicts PM33.7 as written. |
| B3 | **One Application per Flight build.** The Target build binds the P1 demo, so the pulse generator is not a Flight_Build root of the verification job: the job's Link_Checker and Config_Checker steps for Flight builds do not see it. | xtask policy, task 8.4 | Its library crates are marked Flight code (the Profile_Lint and the Target build cover them); `apps/pacemaker/build_checks.py` runs the Link_Checker, the Config_Checker and the stub comparison on both variants; the job's rig-build step does check its rig build. |
| B4 | **The Profile_Lint lints only crates marked for it.** The Therapy_Partition's crate was not linted until it was marked as Flight code; the static checks passed without having looked at it. | xtask `lint_command`, task 8.4 | Marked, and it passes. An earlier note of this plan claimed the lint's verdict for the crate before it had one (corrected under task 8.2). |
| B5 | **The stack of a Partition is the sum of its Tasks' frames.** Four Tasks at four Priorities can all be on the Partition's stack; each holds a copy or two of the step's state. The Link_Checker found a worst case of 4,848 bytes against a declared 4,096. The board runs before that had run on the smaller stack without a fault. | Link_Checker R37.2, task 8.4 | The stack is 8 KiB. The Critical_Section at the Ceiling means the four frames are never all at their deepest together, which the Link_Checker does not know; its bound is the safe one. **Again in task 16.2 (2026-11-24):** the State machine's state (a staged parameter set beside the one in force) took the worst case to 9,632 bytes on the board and 9,784 on QEMU against the 8 KiB declared; the Link_Checker and the Config_Checker refused the build, and the stack is 12 KiB. The rig runs made before the checks were run had passed on the smaller stack; they were repeated. |
| B6 | **A Partition static must be all zero bytes with no padding.** rsk reads every byte of the initial value at compile time (R14.5). | `ResourceCell::for_static`, task 8.3 | Every stored type carries its spare bytes as a named, always-zero field. This is PM30.4 enforced by the build, and the reason the Power-On Reset State is a compile-time fact. |
| B7 | **Timed pacing without a Kernel one-shot.** The compare has to be armed by a Job ahead of time, and getting that right took five corrections to the first design: a guard separate from the lead, the far side kept by a refresh every period, a Pace reported only after its pulse, the sense flag cleared before its compare is written, and a guard of at least a tick. Each was found by a model test or on the board, none by review. | "Arming and the lead", PM27.6 | The scheme works and is measured. The question PM27.6 asks (whether it was awkward enough to justify a Kernel-served one-shot event) is answered so far by that list; the timing error itself is small. To be concluded at the end (task 23.2). |
| B8 | **The Application prover's conventions reach into shared code.** Derived equality needs a model the prover does not derive, a public constant may not be built from a private function, and a bit operation gives it no bound. | task 9.6 | `PartialEq` is derived only outside the prover, two helpers were made public, two bit operations became comparisons. No behaviour changed. |
| B9 | **QEMU has no PPI and one Partition-ownable timer.** | PM26.2, task 7.3 | The board layer for QEMU keeps the register roles in memory; the driver and the step are the board's code. Nothing about widths or latency is shown there. |
| B10 | **A reset through the debug probe is not a clean start.** It restarts the board more than once and halts the core for tens of milliseconds while the timers run; Jobs held up meanwhile then run back to back. | rig, task 7.2 | It produced one false "armed and did not fire" in a sweep, traced with a record of the trial. Measurements now begin after a warm-up, and a trial is settled only when its instant has passed. |
| B12 | **Three sporadic Tasks cannot all have a 2 ms deadline.** The Analyzer charges a Task its Budget and each Job its Kernel entries; with the Application's own measured records one sporadic Job costs 0.48 ms of Kernel time beyond its Budget, so not all three bounds come under 2 ms whatever the Budgets. | task 9.2, PD-O3 | Deadlines of 2, 3 and 4 ms, Budgets of 400 µs, PM-PAR-15 at 4 ms; the Analyzer passes. The longest response measured was 0.3 ms: the gap is the analysis's, and part of it is placeholders. |
| B13 | **rsk's timing measurement is built around its demonstration.** The release-timing run, the cycle test and the stamp probe are configurations of `p1-demo` with the stimulus Application; the trace pins are P1.11 to P1.14, and P1.11 is a pace pin. | task 9.2 | A command of the experiment's own (`cargo xtask pm-measure`) reads the Kernel's record of a 300 s run and writes the records in rsk's format. Δ_entry, Δ_release, Δ_wake and Δ_stamp are not measured for this Application and stay placeholders in its analysis. |
| B14 | **The cycle counter stops when a probe detaches.** A run started with `probe-rs reset` recorded every execution after the first 57 ms as 0 cycles: the detaching probe clears the debug trace enable. | task 9.2, first run | The measurement runs under an attached `probe-rs run`, as rsk's rig does, and a record with Jobs of no execution time is refused. |
| B15 | **The Analyzer charges a fault entry to every Job.** Its per-Job overhead includes Δ_hm; measured on this Application that entry is 91.4 µs with margin, and the stubs another 43.8 µs, where the placeholders were 4.7 µs each. | task 9.8 | The bound for `sense_a` went from 3.400 to 4.036 ms and the result from PASS to FAIL by 36 µs, with nothing in the Application changed and 0.31 ms the longest response seen. The user chose Budgets of 350 µs; measured again, the bounds are 1.690, 2.757 and 3.891 ms and the result a PASS. The Budget is now 116 µs above the longest Job. |
| B16 | **One Kernel parameter cannot be measured from outside.** Δ_release has no record in rsk's own evidence either: its work is inside entries that are measured and charged, and it is charged again at a nominal value. | task 9.8 | Accepted at its nominal value by the user's decision; the Analyzer keeps calling its report no timing evidence. Removing it needs a measurement point in the Kernel. |
| B17 | **A rig scenario per parameter set does not fit cargo features.** Twenty widths, three rates and four Modes would be dozens of features through four manifests. | task 9.4 | The rig builds take the set, and the Heart_Simulator its rhythm, as text from the environment at build time, parsed in a constant function; the record of each run names the text. Flight builds do not have the feature. |
| B18 | **Coverage of a Partition whose logic is in library crates.** rsk instruments Partition crates; this Partition's logic is `pm-core` and `pm-hw`, inlined. | task 9.9 | rsk's coverage build gained a list of further crates. The counters are per build, not per Task, so shared code is attributed to all four Tasks. |
| B19 | **A proof over hardware needs state the prover can see.** The planned contracts on the arming over a register model with a clock could not be put on a trait whose real implementation writes memory-mapped registers and holds no model. | task 12.4, PD-O5 | The order Property P6 asks became a witness type the compiler checks, and the loops' bounds App_Prover variants. The value relations of P6 and P7 stay on the register model of the host tests. |
| B20 | **Dual-chamber timing with Jobs that run after the hardware.** When a Pace fires and another event is handled before its report, the step has to bring the state up to date first, and what that changes must be armed by exactly one later event. | task 11.1 | The arming is the difference an event makes from the accounted state, and the report of an accounted Pace arms what it left. The first version missed one case (the atrial Pace after an accounted ventricular Pace); a generated test that keeps a model of the compares beside the state found it. |
| B21 | **The prover cannot order instants on a counter that wraps.** The program compares two instants by a signed difference; the App_Prover's model of the cast to a signed number has no meaning from 2^31 up, so only one direction of the comparison can be proven. | tasks 12.1, 12.2 | Properties P3, P4 and P8 are proven as stated; of P1 and P2 the parts that are equalities or follow from the provable direction. "No later than the escape interval" and "no earlier than the URL interval" over every event stay with generated tests. **Removed on 2026-11-24:** the therapy logic counts in 64 bits, which do not wrap, and the hardware layer converts at its boundary; an ordering of instants is then a comparison of integers, and P1 and P2 are proven on the step for the ventricle (and P2 for the atrium of an atrial Mode). It cost the driver two functions and three more words of state, the state 60 bytes, and every struct with an instant its spare bytes named again (an instant's alignment is 8). |
| B22 | **One measurement moved the analysis from PASS to FAIL.** The lag from a sense edge to the Kernel's timestamp was 2.4 µs in a thousand edges and 40.1 µs in one (an edge during a Kernel entry). ## D. What task 23.2 asks (PM31.2, PM27.6, PM32.3)

**Every place the source could not be met on rsk or on the Target.** Section A is the list, A1 to A13, with the safe state below it: thirteen places. Six are the Target's (no analog stage, A13; no non-volatile memory, A12; no calendar, A10; the copies of the pace pins, A6; a sense input that is an edge, so no electrogram, A8; no pacing outside the processor, the safe state). Four are of a therapy made of Jobs on a Kernel (A1 the inhibition window, A2 a late Pace, A3 a pulse made by software, A7 a debugger's access). Two are choices of the specification's limits (A5, A11), and one was an omission, repaired since (A9). A4 is closed. Section "A, continued" has the places where the source's tables left a choice, each with who decided it.

**The inhibition window** (A1; PM5.5, PM-PAR-15, PD-04). The source has a Sense cancel the pending Pace. Here the Pace is a compare of a timer, armed ahead; a Sense is an edge that releases a Task, and the Task's Job withdraws the compare. Between the edge and the withdrawal the compare can fire. The requirement therefore states a window, PM-PAR-15, in which the Pace need not be cancelled, and sets it to the sense Task's worst response: 4 ms, from the Analyzer's bounds of task 9.2 (2 ms was the proposal). Measured on the board with nothing else running, the window is 0.14 to 0.22 ms: a ventricular Sense 144 µs before the Pace did not cancel it, one 220 µs before did (task 9.5, 24 runs). A Pace inside the window is delivered and marked as a fusion, the Sense is recorded, and the next interval starts from the Pace. The stated window is some twenty times the measured one because it is a bound for the case that all three sporadic Tasks are released together under the Kernel's worst hold, which no run provoked. A Sense handled in hardware (the edge gating the compare through a PPI channel) would close the window; it would also take the decision out of the proven step, and was not attempted.

**The ownership of P0** (B11, B24; PR-29). On the nRF52840 both GPIO ports are one peripheral, and rsk gives a peripheral one owner. The Therapy_Partition needs the port for the sense, pace and magnet pins, so it owns every pin of the board. Three consequences were met. `therapy` configures the link's three pins for `comms`, for which they mean nothing to it. `comms`'s SPI master can have no chip select: it cannot drive a pin, and a PPI channel from its peripheral to the port would cross Partitions; the bridge frames a transfer by the clock's silence instead (B24). And the electrogram's sampler would have had to be a Task of `therapy` (B46). None was a defect, and one owner a peripheral is what makes the isolation claim simple; but a port is not the unit a board's wiring comes in. A pin-level ownership in rsk's Target table (a mask of the port's pins for each Partition, enforced where the Kernel writes the port's registers for a Partition, as it does for a DMA peripheral) would have removed all three. It was outside this experiment's two permitted changes to rsk.

**Whether timed pacing justified a Kernel one-shot event** (B7, B20; PM27.6). No, by the timing; perhaps, by the cost of getting it right. The scheme, a compare of a Partition-owned timer armed by a Job ahead of time and released from by the pulse's end, gives intervals exact to the timer: every judged Pace of every stage is within 10 µs of its instant against a tolerance of 8 ms, and the pulse widths within a microsecond. The Job's response time bounds only when the arming must be done, not the edge. A one-shot served by the Kernel would time the edge from an interrupt handler and be less exact, by the Kernel's hold and entry (tens of microseconds). What the scheme cost is in B7 and B20: five corrections to the first design before stage E1 paced correctly, a sixth in stage E2 (a Pace accounted before its report, which came back as B45 in stage E5), a hardware layer with its own model tests, and a rule that `control` refreshes every compare each period. Each was found by a model test or on the board, none by review, and none recurred once found. A Kernel one-shot would have removed that work from the Application and put a timed pin event into the Kernel, where it would need its own proof and its own term in the Analyzer; for one Application that is a poor trade. The two things rsk did need were smaller: fixed PPI channels between a Partition's own peripherals, and one more timer in the Target table (Requirement 33).

**Whether seven Tasks sufficed** (PM32.1, PM32.3). Six did. `therapy` has `pace`, `sense_v`, `sense_a` and `control`; `comms` has `link`; `diag` has `history`. The seventh was kept for the electrogram and was not needed when that became a finding (B46). The dispatch vector list was not lengthened, and PM32.4 to PM32.6 did not come into force. What was scarce was not Tasks but Priorities among them and time: three sporadic Tasks at the three highest Priorities cannot all have a deadline of 2 ms (B12), and the response-time bounds of the two sense Tasks have been over their deadlines by the margins since stage E2 (B22). And, in stage E5, the Kernel's RAM in the rig's builds (B44).
