## 2. What the type system gives (R43.3)

Relied on, given sound `unsafe` code and a correct compiler (ASM-14, TBR-21):

- data-race freedom in safe code through `Send` and `Sync`;
- closure-scoped LIFO locking with no escaping references (R8.6), no re-entry (R8.10), and Resource access only by declared Tasks (R8.7), through the `rsk` API shape;
- synchronous Task bodies (PR-09) and, from Phase 4, Endpoint protocol states within a Job (R38.4).

Not given, and allocated in section 3: deadlock freedom, bounded blocking, timing, schedulability, panic freedom, stack bounds, and freedom from leaks.

### 5.1 Bounded blocking: one Critical_Section per Release (R8.4)

Let J be a Job of Priority p released at r, pending but not eligible only because p ≤ System_Ceiling.

1. While J is pending, no Job of Priority below p starts. `Sched::select` takes the highest-Priority pending Release, and if that one is not eligible no pending Release is (proven); a lower-Priority Job is not eligible while p ≤ ceiling.
2. By `lemma_blocked_by_one_holder`, some lock entry with Ceiling ≥ p is held by a Job L of Priority below p, and by step 1 L started before r.
3. At most one such L exists. If L₁ below L₂ on the stack both held Resources with Ceiling ≥ p, then L₂ started above the ceiling of that instant (invariant 1), which is at least the Ceiling of L₁'s Resource (invariant 4), so Priority(L₂) > p, a contradiction.
4. When L's outermost unlock drops the ceiling below p, `apply` pends J's vector at once (A4, P-04) and J preempts L before L executes again, so L cannot lock a second Resource with Ceiling ≥ p in J's way.

So J waits for at most one outermost Critical_Section (with its nested sections) of one lower-Priority Job, plus Kernel overhead. The PBT against the SRP simulator (task 15.4, 2026-10-23: `crates/rsk-analyzer/tests/properties.rs`, Property 18) observes no response time above the Analyzer's bound on any of more than 100 passing random sets, with and without injected Overruns; see 5.10.

**Mechanized 2026-10-23 (task 4.12), in four lemmas.** Every step of the ghost history now records the executing Task after it (`top`); `history_wf` relates consecutive tops by the step's kind (`top_rel`: a dispatch makes its Task the top and, as `Kernel::dispatch` requires, that Task was eligible after the previous step, above the recorded ceiling and the recorded top; a lock or unlock is by the top and keeps it); `Kernel::inv` ties the last recorded top to the scheduler (`top_match`). The premise of the run-level lemmas is that J waits: its Release is step `r`, its dispatch (if any) step `d`, and after no step before `d - 1` is J eligible on the recorded ceiling and top (`eligible_after`); that premise is the Kernel_Arch promptness of A4 (an eligible Job is pended at once, `apply_core`) together with J's Release staying pending, and is the caller's. Step 1: `history::lemma_no_lower_dispatch_while_waiting`, no Job of Priority below p starts in the window. Step 3: `Sched::lemma_one_low_blocker`, at most one Job of Priority below p holds an entry with Ceiling at least p, in every state. Step 4: `history::lemma_no_new_blocking_section`, a lock in the window by a Job of Priority below p finds the System_Ceiling already at least p (so it opens no new blocking section), and `Sched::lemma_low_locker_nested`, in that state the locker itself holds the entry that blocks J (so the lock is nested in its own open section). Step 2 is `lemma_blocked_by_one_holder` as before. Together: the only blocking section is the one open at J's Release, held by one lower-Priority Job, and it is never followed by another before J starts. What remains outside the theorems is the premise above and the Kernel overhead term. The mutations `lock-by-other-than-top` and `dispatch-below-ceiling` fail steps 4 and 1.

### 5.2 Lazy release of killed Jobs delays nothing (R8.9, R14.5)

`Sched::kill` marks a preempted Job K but leaves its lock entries until Kernel_Arch unwinds to it, when `finish_top` releases them. Every Job above K started above the ceiling of its start instant (invariant 1), which covers the Ceilings of K's Resources (invariant 4). A Job X blocked only by K's retained Resources has Priority at most their Ceiling, so below the executing Job's Priority, and is not eligible while any Job above K is in progress. When the last Job above K ends, the unwind reaches K and releases its entries in the same Kernel entry (`epilogue`). The retained entries therefore never delay a Job that the Jobs above K would not delay anyway. Assumes A1 and the unwind order of the stubs (UJ-023, review).

**Mechanized 2026-10-22 (task 4.13):** `Sched::lemma_lazy_release_delays_nothing`, the logic content of the argument as a theorem over any state satisfying `Sched::inv`: with Job K at stack position `k` killed and some Job in progress above it, a pending Task X that the System_Ceiling blocks although no entry of a holder other than K blocks it has Priority below every Job above K, the executing one included, so X is not eligible whether or not K's entries are released. The proof uses the attained maximum of the mirror (`lemma_max_ceiling_attained`), invariant 4 (K's entries have Ceilings at most the start ceiling of each Job above K), and invariant 1. The mutation `lazy-release-no-job-above` (dropping the Job above K) fails it. What stays outside the theorem: that the unwind to K releases the entries in the same Kernel entry, which is `epilogue_core` (Verification_Plan section 4) and the stub order UJ-023.

### 5.3 Ceiling restore across preemptions (R8.2)

`Sched::unlock` proves that after an unlock the ceiling is the maximum over the remaining entries. The remaining entries are exactly those present before the matching lock:

- `lock` pushes at the top and leaves the entries below untouched (proven);
- entries are ordered by holder (invariant 3), so a Job that preempts and later ends removes only its own entries (`complete` and `end_job` leave `lock_res` and `lock_depth` unchanged and shrink `lock_len` only by the top's entries, proven);
- an unlock succeeds only on the top entry owned by the executing Job (proven).

Hence the ceiling after the unlock equals its value before the matching lock.

**Mechanized 2026-10-22 (task 4.13):** `logic::history::lemma_ceiling_restored`. Every step of the ghost history carries a snapshot of the lock stack and the System_Ceiling after it; `history_wf` relates consecutive snapshots by the step's kind (`step_rel`: a lock pushes one entry, an unlock pops the top one, a Job end drops a suffix, the rest keep the stack) and ties each ceiling to its snapshot (`ceiling_of`); `Kernel::inv` ties the last snapshot to the scheduler (`locks_match`), which every entry point maintains and `record` re-establishes from the `Sched` postconditions (`lemma_rel_shrink`, `lemma_rel_lock`, `lemma_rel_unlock`). The theorem: for a lock step `i` and the matching unlock `j` (the first later step at which the stack is back to its depth before `i`, the stack never having gone below the locked entry in between), the stack after `j` is the stack before `i` and the ceiling after `j` equals the ceiling before `i`, whatever other Jobs locked, unlocked, started, or ended in between. The mutation `history-end-forgets-prefix` (a Job end allowed to change entries below the released suffix) fails it. This row of section 3 is now proof evidence at the logic level.

### 5.4 MIT spacing over a run (R10.1, R10.2)

Every accepted Release of a Sporadic_Task sets `last_due` to its own due instant (`accepted`, `deferred_release`, proven). Acceptance requires `now ≥ last_due + MIT` and a deferred Release becomes due at exactly `last_due + MIT` (proven, `Kernel::inv`). Every entry point ensures `mit_spaced`: `last_due` is never forgotten and moves only by at least one MIT (proven per entry point; restarts keep it, R14.5). By induction over the sequence of entry points, consecutive accepted Releases become due at least one MIT apart. `lemma_mit_spaced_trans` is the inductive step for one composition.

**Mechanized 2026-10-22 (task 4.14):** `logic::history::lemma_mit_spacing_over_run`. The trace invariant `history_wf` now states `releases_spaced`: any two Release steps of one Sporadic_Task in the history are due at least one MIT apart. `Kernel::inv` ties the history to the MIT state (`releases_linked`: for a Sporadic_Task, every recorded Release is due no later than `Timing::last_due`, which, once set, is the due instant of a recorded Release); `record` re-establishes both for a Release step from the acceptance rule of `Timing::accept` (`now >= last_due + MIT`) or the deferral clause of `inv` (`deferred_until == last_due + MIT`), through `lemma_release_spaced` and `lemma_release_match_kept`. The theorem reads the invariant back for any two Release steps `i < j` of a Sporadic_Task: `h[j].arg >= h[i].arg + MIT`, so fewer than two Releases of the Task become due in any window shorter than the MIT. The induction the text describes is the one `record` performs at every step. The mutation `mit-run-spacing-dropped` (the invariant weakened to ordering) fails the theorem.

### 5.5 Deadlock freedom (R8.5)

A lock never waits: its outcome depends only on the accessor check, re-entry, and capacity, never on another holder (`lemma_no_other_holder`, proven). No Job suspends inside a Critical_Section (PR-08, PR-09). There is therefore no wait-for edge between Jobs; a pending Job waits only for higher-Priority Jobs to end, which Budget enforcement bounds (P-17).

**Mechanized 2026-10-22 (task 4.14):** `Sched::lemma_deadlock_free`, for every state satisfying `Sched::inv` (so for every state of a run). The waiting relation of the Kernel is `blocked_by(t, i)`: the pending Job of Task `t`, with no Job of `t` in progress and not eligible, is kept from starting by the Job at stack position `i`, which is the executing Job with Priority at least `t`'s or holds a Resource whose Ceiling is at least `t`'s Priority. The theorem states (1) a lock never waits for another Job (`lemma_no_other_holder`, restated), (2) every pending non-eligible Job either waits for its Task's own Job in progress (R9.7) or has a `blocked_by` blocker (the executing Job, or the holder of the entry that attains the System_Ceiling, by `lemma_max_ceiling_attained`), and (3) a blocker is in progress and is blocked by nothing, so every wait-for path has length one and no cycle exists. Not part of the theorem: that waiting ends, which is Budget enforcement (P-17) and the Jobs above ending (`epilogue_core`). The mutation `deadlock-equal-priority-unblocked` (a blocker must have strictly higher Priority) fails clause (2) for a pending Job of the executing Job's Priority. Note the correction to the text above: the blocked Job's Priority may exceed the holder's (bounded inversion); the theorem states the blocker by Ceiling, not by Priority.

### 5.6 Panic freedom under abort (ORQ-07)

- `arch`, `arch/cm4`, and `rsk-entry`: safe Rust outside `verus!`, with ordinary indexing and arithmetic. It is not proven panic-free. A panic there reaches the single panic handler and the system safe state (R14.4), so it is a detected Kernel fault, not undefined behaviour.
- The Link_Checker's no-panic-path check (task 12.3, 2026-10-23) shows that no Kernel code calls `panic_fmt` or `panic` explicitly (a violation if it did, PR-19) and lists the implicit checks the compiler kept: `panic_bounds_check` reachable through 21 Kernel functions and `panic_const_rem_by_zero` through 2. ### 5.8 Stale pending dispatch bits (A4)

When a Release is suppressed after its vector was pended, the NVIC may still take that vector. Taking it clears the pending bit; `irq_entry` finds the Task not eligible and returns unchanged, and the NVIC arbitrates again among the remaining pending vectors, where P-04 applies. Each stale bit is consumed once, so a suppression event causes at most NT stale entries, each costing one stub entry. Whether the Analyzer's Kernel overhead terms include that cost is to be confirmed (task 15.2).

### 5.8a A dispatch vector taken inside another handler's return window (the INVPC of 2026-11-06)

The dispatch stub keeps PRIMASK set from its entry to the instruction before its exception return, and clears it there because a Job runs in Thread mode with PRIMASK clear and an exception return does not restore PRIMASK. Between that clear and the return there is a window of one instruction in which a pending exception of higher priority is taken: at the Kernel level, the time base or an intake, which never dispatch themselves and return into the window; or a dispatch vector of a higher level, which until 2026-11-06 dispatched its Job from there and returned to Thread mode with the preempted dispatch still active. DD-01 as revised forbids exactly that (no Job executes with an exception active; NONBASETHRDENA stays clear), and the processor refuses the return with UFSR.INVPC: the UsageFault of section 13, reproduced three times under the timing configuration and diagnosed on 2026-11-06 by the fault record's processor state (ICSR with RETTOBASE clear, NVIC_IABR0 naming the other Partition's dispatch vector as active, CCR 0x200). The interleaving needs an intake at the Kernel level to have preempted the window first and pended the higher dispatch vector, which the NVIC then takes before the preempted stub's own return; it happened about once an hour under the timing configuration, whose `pulse` edge every 18 ms meets `filter`'s and `control`'s dispatches, and never under the others.

**The first fix was wrong, and the witness build found it (2026-11-07).** The fix of 2026-11-06 made a dispatch vector whose preempted context is a handler (EXC_RETURN bit 3 clear) re-pend itself and return unchanged, expecting the NVIC to take it again after the preempted stub's return. Two hours of the fixed binary ran clean but recorded no entry into the window, so a witness build was made: the rsk-kernel feature `defer-probe` spins in the window (about 150 µs then, 30 µs now; `cargo xtask hil --release-timing --short --defer-probe`), so that the interleaving occurs many times a run instead of once an hour. Its first run showed the re-pend to be a livelock: the re-pended vector has a higher priority than the preempted stub's active exception, so the processor takes it again on the nested return, before the stub's `bx`, and again, 362,283 entries in 0.7 s until the run broke down. A re-pend can never work in the real one-instruction window either; it had simply never run.

**The return window: FAULTMASK closes it.** The stub now executes `cpsid f` before `cpsie i`: FAULTMASK raises the execution priority to -1, so nothing but NMI is taken between the PRIMASK clear and the `bx`, and the exception return itself clears FAULTMASK (ARMv7-M B1.4.3: every exception return other than NMI's), which an exception return does not do for PRIMASK. The pending vector is then taken from Thread mode, after the stub's exception is inactive (or tail-chains from the return, with the frame on the PSP and EXC_RETURN naming Thread mode: the same). The fence is in the dispatch return, the no-change return, and `rsk_unwind`'s return (a dispatch stub that ended a Job unwinds from there at its own level); the Kernel-level handlers need none, since no dispatch vector can preempt them. The cost is one instruction per return. A fault inside the fenced window would escalate to HardFault under FAULTMASK and lock up; the window holds `cpsie i` and the return only, and the return's EXC_RETURN is the plan's constant (the Verus postcondition of `irq_entry_core`), so none is possible there.

**The entry instruction is the other window, and the records' refresh found it (2026-11-07).** With the return fenced, two of the day's four coverage runs (the timing configuration's and the Kernel's) still entered the backstop once each, at a release instant of `control`, whose dispatch vector is the highest. A stub's first instruction is `cpsid i`, and nothing masks a pending exception before it: the exception entry of a dispatch stub A of level L completes with PRIMASK clear, and a vector pended during A's entry (the time base, releasing `control` at that instant, having preempted A there and returned into it) with a priority above L is taken before `cpsid i` executes, with A active and no instruction of A run. That is the interleaving of the finding (the third reproduction's record: `filter`'s vector active with `control`'s dispatching), not the return window, which PRIMASK made a one-instruction target and FAULTMASK closes; the entry window has no instruction to put a mask in front of. **The deferral.** A dispatch vector that finds a handler preempted (EXC_RETURN bit 3 clear) disables itself in the NVIC, records its number in `ArchState::deferred` (eight slots, one per level at most; a ninth is the backstop's Kernel fault, R16.5), traces `window`, counts (`ARCH_WINDOWS`), and returns unchanged into A's entry. A then runs under PRIMASK as every entry does, and the first thing `irq_entry_core` does for an entry taken from Thread mode (bit 3 set: A, never a nested entry, which keeps a vector deferred by a nested entry from being re-taken at A's entry again) is to re-enable and re-pend every deferred vector; PRIMASK holds them to A's return, after which the NVIC takes them from the base level in priority order, where `control`'s dispatch preempts A's Job as the SRP intends. The Kernel's state is untouched by a deferral (the Release stays pending in the NVIC, eligible or not); a Release the Kernel suppresses meanwhile is the stale-bit case of 5.8 when the vector is re-taken. Proven: `irq_entry_core`'s postconditions hold through both loops (Verus); the disable and the re-enable are `Action::Disable` and `Action::Enable` of the Hardware_Model, as the boot's.

**The entry instruction's third path: a Kernel-level entry that ends a Job there (the same day, the witness build's first run with the deferral).** The time base or an intake can preempt a dispatch stub at its entry instruction as well (they return into it, so no deferral is needed), but when such an entry ends the executing Job by a response (a deadline miss or Overrun of `sensor` under the timing configuration's injections) and unwinds, `rsk_unwind` expects the MSP top to be the ended Job's level record (the dispatch stub's pushes, Property 2's LIFO) and finds instead the entry's own exception frame, since the preempted stub has pushed nothing yet; it pops those words as the record and returns to Thread mode with the preempted stub active: UFSR.INVPC again, and the fault record of that run showed it (the preempted stub's vector active, the frame's words garbage). The fixed Kernel owes the unwind to the preempted stub: `irq_entry_core` keeps the level (`ArchState::pending_unwind`, traced as `window` with the level) and returns unchanged; the preempted stub, once it has pushed its own record, pops it, unwinds the owed level, and re-pends its own vector, which the NVIC takes from the resumed context, where it dispatches. The SVC and fault handlers are entered from Thread mode only and never owe one.

**The witness (2026-11-07, `docs/hil/findings/defer_{probe,nofix}_nrf52840_timing_short/`).** The rsk-kernel feature `defer-probe` spins about 30 µs at the stub's entry instruction and 30 µs in its return window (`cargo xtask hil --release-timing --short --defer-probe`), so that both interleavings occur many times a run instead of about once an hour; `defer-nofix` removes the fence and the deferral from the same build. Without them: the first UsageFault 141 ms into the run, 340 fault-record entries (every held one UFSR.INVPC with the diagnostics of the finding), the run broken down in 61 s: the defect reproduced at will. With them: 189 entry-window occurrences in 180 s, each handled (`control`'s dispatch vector deferred and re-taken from Thread mode; `sampler`'s level unwound by the preempted stub on the time base's behalf), no fault, 9,119 pulses answered and none late, 2,184 Kernel actions recorded; the run's one failure is a deadline miss of `control` at 20,540 ms, identical to the cycle in two builds of the witness (the 60 µs the spins add to every dispatch move a `control` Release into `sensor`'s Critical_Section at that phase), a perturbation of the probe and not of the Kernel, which froze the trace as any unexpected event does. The widened windows are charged to the dispatched Job and perturb the timing (the step's bounds are reported, not judged, and no record is written from a witness run); the fence and the deferral check, two instructions and a compare, are inside the Δ_entry the cycle configuration measures, and the records were refreshed on the fixed Kernel. The hour-long and short runs of the campaign stay the evidence that the Flight stub meets no fault; the witness is the evidence that both windows are handled, and the rig's step "dispatch deferrals (5.8a)" reports the deferrals of every timing and stress run, each a handled occurrence of the interleaving. **The campaign of 2026-11-07 (daytime) recorded the first natural occurrences on a Flight stub: four in the hour-long stress run, every one handled, the run passing every rig step with no fault; the hour-long timing run recorded none.**

### 6.4 Interleaving tests: Loom and Shuttle (R47; task 17.2, 2026-10-24)

The Host_Test_Harness is the crate `rsk-loom` (host only, never in a Flight_Build). Its model, `Machine`, runs the Kernel's portable logic (`rsk_logic::logic::kernel::Kernel`, the same source the Flight_Build compiles, here for the host; not a reimplementation, R47.2) and represents interrupt preemption by threads constrained to the Hardware_Model's rules: the time base's thread delivers compare matches (`Kernel::timer_event`, as `timer_core` does), a Release_Source's thread delivers intakes of a Sporadic_Task (`Kernel::intake`), and Thread mode's thread runs the executing Job's program (`svc_lock`, `svc_unlock`, the return into `job_return`, as `svc_core` does). Each is one Kernel entry, atomic as on the Target (PRIMASK or BASEPRI at the Kernel level while the Kernel runs, A2; non-reentrant, A10), so the explorer interleaves whole entries. After every entry the model does what `apply_core` does (BASEPRI from `basepri_for`, the selected Task's vector pended) and what the NVIC does under HW-05: it takes the pending dispatch vector of the Task whose Priority exceeds the executing Job's and the System_Ceiling (`lemma_nvic_takes_iff_above_job_and_ceiling`) and runs the dispatch stub's entry (`eligible`, then `Kernel::dispatch`), repeating while a vector is takeable. Since the Kernel pends only the Task it selects (revised DD-01), the NVIC rule is a cross-check of `Kernel::select`, not an independent source of dispatches: the model asserts the two name the same Task.

Every explored execution is checked after every entry against the Verus postconditions restated in Rust (R47.2): `Sched::inv` (Invariants 1 to 4 of design.md: the stack of Jobs in progress strictly increasing in Priority, each started above the System_Ceiling of its instant, the lock stack held by Jobs in progress that may access its Resources and ordered by holder, Resources held beneath a Job with Ceilings at most its start Ceiling, the System_Ceiling the maximum Ceiling held), and per entry the observable clauses of each operation's `ensures`: a dispatched Task was eligible and is the SRP selection (R7.1 to R7.3), may access no Resource a preempted Job holds (R8.3), and is pushed as executing; a lock is taken exactly when the executing Job may access the Resource, does not hold it, and the lock stack has room, and raises the Ceiling to the Resource's (R8.1, R8.7, R8.10); an unlock restores the previous Ceiling (R8.2); a return ends the executing Job holding nothing (R7.5, R8.9); a killed Job is unwound at once as `end_plan_core` does; a timer event or an intake starts no Job except through a dispatch; a second intake within the MIT never duplicates a pending Release (R10.3); and once the arbitration settles no eligible Release is left pending (Property 1).

Configurations and runs (`cargo test -p rsk-loom`, part of the job's workspace tests): the small configuration (three Tasks, two Resources of Ceiling 5, two Partitions, one Sporadic_Task of Priority 7; Task programs that nest Critical_Sections and one that attempts a Resource it may not access) under Loom, exhaustively for the time base against Thread mode (two compare matches: the first Releases, then the second period falling anywhere in the Jobs' steps, Critical_Sections included; 1,386 executions, 0.2 s) and for a Release_Source against Thread mode (two intakes within one MIT; 5,373 executions, 0.7 s), and for the three sources together under Loom's preemption bound of 3 (65,339 executions, 13 s); the large configuration (five Tasks, three Resources of Ceilings 3, 6, 7, two Sporadic_Tasks, a Task accessing Resources of different Ceilings) under Shuttle, 20,000 random schedules of four compare matches and three intakes per Sporadic_Task (4 s). Each run asserts that its executions include preemptions (R7.1), Releases held back by a Ceiling (R8.3), and refused locks (R8.7), so a run that explored nothing of interest fails.

Time limit (R47.1): a Loom run must complete within 120 s on the development machine; the unbounded space of the three sources together does not (the first attempt, before the drivers were corrected to lock once per Kernel entry, ran 3.6 million executions in 10 minutes for one tick and two intakes), so the three-source run is bounded and the large configuration goes to Shuttle. Validation of the harness (as section 9.2 does for the proofs): two mutations of the Kernel logic replayed under it, `Sched::lock` leaving the System_Ceiling unchanged (caught by every run at the lock postcondition) and `Sched::select` ignoring the System_Ceiling (caught by the exhaustive time-base run and the bounded run at the selection check, "select chose an ineligible Task"); two mutations of the model's own NVIC rule (ignoring the executing Job's level; skipping the eligibility guard) were not caught, which records that under DD-01 the model's arbitration is downstream of `Kernel::select` and not load-bearing, as the first paragraph says. A finding of the same session: a driver that held the model's lock across a Job's whole program explored almost nothing (the events could land only while Thread mode was idle) and let the `select` mutation through; the drivers lock once per entry.

Limitations recorded (R47.3): Loom explores the interleavings of the model's synchronisation operations under the sequentially consistent semantics of its primitives, not every C11 behaviour, and here only the orderings of whole Kernel entries, which is the Target's granularity by A2 and A10; Loom's preemption bound makes the three-source run incomplete by construction; Shuttle is randomized and not sound (RN-37). The model abstracts the Kernel_Arch sequence inside an entry (BASEPRI written before the vector is pended, the exception frame, the MPU view), which Verus covers (`apply_core`, A1 to A11), and time: the time base advances to the next armed instant only. Every result is testing evidence. ### 7.1 Aeneas evaluation on one pure control law (task 17.3, R46.5; 2026-10-26)

**What was done.** `proofs/aeneas/controllaw` is a dependency-free `no_std` crate with one pure control law of the shape a level A Task body computes between its Critical_Sections: a fixed-point PI step (`pi_step`: the error clamped to 2^16, the integral accumulated with an anti-windup clamp at 2^22, the proportional term `KP * e >> 15` in `i32`, the integral term `KI * integral >> 15` in `i64` then cast back, the output saturated at 2^17; Q15 gains). Charon (Aeneas's rustc driver, pinned nightly-2026-09-17) translates the crate to LLBC and Aeneas to Lean (`proofs/aeneas/lean/ControlLaw/Controllaw.lean`, 115 lines, generated and committed): a pure functional model in the `Result` monad in which every `+`, `*`, shift, and `as` is a possible `fail`. The handwritten proofs (`Proofs.lean`, 97 lines) establish for every state satisfying the invariant `|integral| ≤ 2^22` and every error that `pi_step` returns `ok` (no panic, no arithmetic overflow, every cast in bounds), that the output lies in `[-2^17, 2^17]`, and that the new state satisfies the invariant; `clamp` is specified once and reused; the initial state satisfies the invariant. `lake build` checks them in the job's step "Aeneas evaluation" (`proof-app-aeneas`, skipped with a warning where Lean is absent); the translation is committed, so the job needs Lean and the pinned checkout's Lean library, not Charon or Aeneas.

**Toolchain and pins** (toolchain/decision.toml `[aeneas]`): Aeneas commit 472b3d5 and Charon commit 40ca4ab6c (neither is a release), built from a checkout next to the repository as Creusot is (OCaml 5.4.1 through opam, Charon in Rust on nightly-2026-09-17), Lean v4.35.0 through elan, Mathlib at the revision Aeneas's manifest pins (7.6 GB of build artifacts fetched from Mathlib's cache). The install took about an hour of machine time against Creusot's comparable effort; the dependency chain (two translators, two language toolchains, Mathlib) is the heaviest of the tools evaluated.

**Findings.** (1) The translation is immediate and readable; the Rust had to avoid a type named `Pi` (Aeneas refuses the clash with Mathlib's `Pi`) and the `Debug`, `PartialEq`, `Eq` derives (they translate, but pull `core::fmt` models into the file), keeping `Clone, Copy`. (2) The `step` tactic (the renamed `progress`) discharges most obligations with the library's `@[step]` lemmas and `scalar_tac`; three things needed a hand: the constants defined by shifts (`1 << 16`) are `Result` values proven by `rfl`, the shifts' postconditions are `Int` shifts rewritten to divisions by `Int.shiftRight_eq_div_pow` before `omega` can bound them, and the narrowing `i64 → i32` cast needs the in-bounds lemma `IScalar.cast_inBounds_spec` named explicitly with its bound proven first, because the default step leaves only `i10 = IScalar.cast ...` without a value. The proof of a 7-line function is some 45 tactic lines, written in about an hour with the library's source as the reference. (3) Aeneas does not translate floating point (no `f32` in its scalar types), so the demo's `lowpass` is out of reach for it as it is for Creusot (RN-10). (4) Closures, `&mut` through the `rsk` API, and loops were not exercised; the design's assessment (limited loop and closure support, RN-21) stands. (5) Trust: Charon as a rustc driver on a nightly later than the Flight_Build's 1.95.0 (ORQ-08 as for Creusot), Aeneas's translation (OCaml, unverified), and the Lean kernel with Mathlib; no DO-330 qualification exists for any of them; no credit is claimed.

**Conclusion.** Aeneas fits the role the design gives it: small pure computations (control laws, validation functions of R42.4) for which a Lean-level functional proof, possibly against Mathlib, is wanted, as substitute evidence under R46.5 where the App_Prover cannot express a construct. It does not replace Creusot for Task bodies: Creusot proves the demo's bodies against the `rsk` API's contracts with no manual proof text, where Aeneas needs the proof written by hand and its translation needs the code shaped for it. The designation of section 7 stands.

### 8.7 UPPAAL cross-check (R31, R32.6, ORQ-22; task 16, 2026-10-26)

The UPPAAL_Exporter (`rsk-analyzer::uppaal`) writes, for every analysis, the model (`model.xml`, UPPAAL's XML) and the query file (`model.q`; R31.1). The model (`rsk-uppaal/2`): one release generator per Task, periodic (exactly every period) or sporadic (at least the MIT after the previous release, any later instant), each followed by a jitter of up to J_rel; one Task automaton per Task with the SRP start condition (no Job of higher or equal Priority and lower identifier executing, Priority above the System_Ceiling; ties by R7.3), a blocking interval of up to B_i before the Job starts, a stopwatch execution bounded by E_i (Budget plus the overheads of R29.1) that progresses only while the Task executes, resumption of the highest-Priority preempted Task on completion, and a deadline-miss location (R31.2); the query `A[] not (T0.Missed || ...)` is satisfied exactly when no deadline-miss location is reachable (R31.3); every automaton's Task_Model identifier is recorded in the declaration (R31.7); the reader `read_export` recovers each Task's Priority, period or MIT, deadline, and execution-time bound (R31.8; Property 15, tested). Two approximations are recorded in every report's omitted terms (R31.2), and a third since 2026-11-06 where a Sporadic_Task exists (the intake term of R19.2 on the Tasks above the source's Task is in the RTA only): Critical_Sections and the System_Ceiling are represented by the blocking interval, not by locations that raise the ceiling, and the Kernel overheads of R29.3 are folded into E_i rather than modelled as locations. Where `verifyta` is installed the Analyzer runs the query under a time limit and an address-space limit (`--uppaal-timeout`, `--uppaal-memory`; R31.9), records the verifier's verdict and version in the report, and classifies the result against the RTA verdict (R31.4): agreement; pessimism (RTA FAIL, no miss reachable; R31.6); discrepancy (a reachable miss while RTA PASS; the trace is attached and marked as requiring confirmation, because stopwatch models are verified by over-approximation; R31.5); inconclusive (limit exceeded or no verdict; R31.9). A verifier that is absent leaves the status `not_run`, which is never counted as agreement.

The corpus (R32.6, PAR-03): `cargo xtask uppaal-corpus` generates 50 Task_Models deterministically from synthetic App_Declarations through the Generator itself (2 to 7 Tasks; PAR-03 proposes up to 8, but the Target tables offer 7 free dispatch vectors, DD-01; two Partitions; Priorities 1 to 7 with ties; periods 5 to 200 ms; deadlines at or below the period; up to two Resources per Partition with random accessors; about a third of the Tasks sporadic, released by Release_Signals), analyses each with placeholder timing, runs the cross-check where the verifier exists, and writes `docs/uppaal_corpus_report.md`; the verification job runs the same corpus (step "UPPAAL cross-check corpus", Evidence_Item `check-uppaal-corpus`), requires the committed report to equal the regenerated one, and gives the item `pass` only when no cross-check is a discrepancy without a disposition, `fail` on an undisposed discrepancy, and `inconclusive` when the verifier is absent or a run hit its limits. Dispositions are recorded in `docs/uppaal_dispositions.toml`, one per model, and the report prints them beside the discrepancy; this is the record R32.6 asks for. Status at 2026-10-26: UPPAAL is not installed on the development machine (a licensed download the project has not made), so the 50 exports exist, the RTA verdicts are 11 PASS and 39 FAIL, every cross-check is `not_run`, the item is `inconclusive`, and no discrepancy has been seen or disposed; the model's semantics have been checked by its reader and by inspection only, which is a limitation to clear on the first machine with UPPAAL (ORQ-22, GAP-19). The `-t0` option asks the verifier for the shortest trace on a reachable miss.

## 10. HAL and PAC strategy for Partitions (R58.3, ORQ-21)

- Partition crates keep `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` (PR-20); the Build_System checks every crate root.
- Peripheral access goes through rsk drivers over the PAC. Third-party crates with `unsafe` (PAC, HAL) are allowed as dependencies of a Partition only with a Trust_Base_Register entry per crate and version (R58.2, R58.5, TBR-13); the dependency gate enforces the entries.
- The MPU confines their effects to the Partition's own peripheral windows (P-16). The Generator places only declared, Partition-owned peripherals in windows and rejects Kernel-owned ones (PR-29); since 2026-11-05 the layout also carries the PPB and every Kernel-owned peripheral of the Target table as Kernel peripheral intervals, which the boot validation keeps apart from every Partition interval and the proof keeps outside every view (R16.2), and the Config_Checker compares with its own table and with the linked binary's `LAYOUT` image (R16.7).
- EasyDMA pointer and length registers are written only through the Kernel's mediation service (DD-08, task 5.5, built 2026-10-23: `rsk::dma_request` over SVC 6 and `dma_request_core`), because a DMA master bypasses the MPU (ORQ-03). The service accepts a channel of the executing Job's own Partition and a buffer in that Partition's RAM above its stack region and within enabled subregions (R17.1, `PartitionLayout::dma_permitted`); anything else is refused before any register is written and applies the Partition's fault response (R17.2, `EV_DMA_REJECTED`). List-mode registers are cleared at boot and never written; PPI channels are cleared at boot; a stop or restart triggers every stop task of the Partition's channels before its memory is re-initialized (R17.6). The channel table is Generated_Config's `DMA` (one entry per channel of every EasyDMA-capable peripheral a Partition owns, from the Target table's register offsets).
- The p1-demo uses no peripherals, so no HAL or PAC crate is in the Flight_Build graph yet.

### 11.1 First results on the nRF52840-DK (2026-10-29)

- **HFCLK.** The Kernel starts the crystal oscillator at boot (DD-05: the time base runs from it); the RC oscillator's HFCLKSTAT.STATE already reads running, so the boot waits on EVENTS_HFCLKSTARTED. Before the fix, boot check bit 10 (R12.1, ASM-05) failed and the safe state reported it: the check does its job.
- **Access port protection.** This silicon enables APPROTECT at every reset. A HIL build (feature `debug-port`, never a Flight_Build feature) writes APPROTECT.DISABLE first thing at boot; the persistent UICR.APPROTECT = HwDisabled is the operator's one-time setting. A Flight_Build leaves the port protected, which is the production setting.
- **Watchdog path.** The first full run locked up and the Kernel-owned watchdog reset the chip; the next boot logged EV_WATCHDOG_RESET, boot check bit 7, EV_SAFE_STATE, and reported through the semihosting console as ORQ-31 specifies. `hil-watchdog`'s claim is observed, not yet as a designed test.
- **The "lockup" was the time base.** The nRF TIMER0 compare is absolute: a compare instant the counter has already passed matches only after the 32-bit wrap, and the Kernel's minimum compare distance of 2 ticks (125 ns) is below the time the write itself takes, so the first compare (instant 0) never fired, no tick fed the watchdog, and the watchdog reset the chip. The nRF driver now pends the time-base interrupt when the instant has passed by the time the compare is written; the QEMU timer is armed with a relative distance and never showed it (HW-12 holds for both, by different means).
- **Semihosting halts the core.** Every semihosting call stops the CPU while the probe services it, with the time base running; three boot trace lines consumed the 200 ms operating duration, and the Tasks' one-byte prints would have cost Budgets and deadlines. HIL builds carry the `quiet` feature (no prints during the run; the report after the operating end is unaffected), and the Kernel's `trace` feature is unusable on a board under a watchdog.
- **A real MPU caught a real layout defect.** The Job wrappers of `echo` and `pulse`, small once quiet, were copied by cross-crate inlining into the system crate's object and placed in `.kernel.text`; the Jobs fetched Kernel code and the MPU raised an instruction access violation (CFSR.IACCVIOL) at each release, each a Partition restart. QEMU never faulted. The Link_Checker's R16.3 check reports exactly this on the board's binary (two violations), which the job had not run on that binary; the Generator now emits `#[inline(never)]` on every Job wrapper, and the check is clean. Diagnosed through a new Kernel record of the first MemManage fault (CFSR and the stacked PC, printed by the demo's report).
- **Durations in ticks are board-specific.** The demo's busy waits were tuned for the QEMU board's 25 MHz time base and 250,000-iteration spin; on the nRF52840 `log` overran its Budget and `sampler` starved. The Generator now emits each Partition's `rsk_target::{TICK_HZ, CPU_HZ, ms}` and the demo states its waits in milliseconds.
- **The debugger-attached event is information**, not a fault, in the demo's pass criterion (ORQ-30); QEMU never raised it.
- **Bench procedure.** After a power cycle or any reset outside a probe session the access port is locked; the operator unlocks it once with probe-rs's `--allow-erase-all` run (the board holds nothing else), after which the HIL build's APPROTECT.DISABLE write at boot and the fed watchdog in the safe state keep the port open between runs, and `cargo xtask hil` attaches without erasing. UICR.APPROTECT = HwDisabled was written as well but did not by itself open the port on this silicon. **2026-11-06:** both are needed on this silicon. A `--allow-erase-all` unlock erases the UICR too, after which every HIL run left the port locked at its reboot although it wrote APPROTECT.DISABLE; restoring UICR.APPROTECT = HwDisabled (0x5A at 0x10001208, programmed through probe-rs's flash loader: `probe-rs download --binary-format bin --base-address 0x10001208` of the four bytes `5A 00 00 00`) made the runs keep the port open again. A probe-rs run killed mid-way leaves the board locked; the rig's runs are left to end.
- **Open.** Running the Link_Checker, Config_Checker, and Analyzer on the HIL binary in the job (the Link_Checker was run by hand); `hil-watchdog`, `hil-boot-checks` as designed tests (both behaviours observed). The Build_Manifest entry for the HIL build was added with the rig (section 11.2).

**The rig.** Three PCA10056 v3.0.3 boards (nRF52840 AAF0) and an fx2lafw logic analyzer (Saleae Logic compatible, 8 channels, 24 MHz at most) driven by sigrok-cli 0.7.2, described in `hil-rig.toml` at the workspace root (schema `rsk-hil-rig/1`): the target board A, the stimulus board B, and the spare C (listed, not wired). **The Build_Manifest records the HIL builds** (`hil_binaries`: package, features, hash over the loadable image, loadable bytes, Generated_Config checksum), built in the manifest step whether or not a board is attached; the rig reads back every loadable part of each image from the board after flashing (the target's after its run, because probe-rs `run` is the flashing step and `attach` does not reset) and hashes the read-back exactly as the manifest hashes the ELF, so the two agree only when every byte agrees (R48.4).

**The self-test** (the evidence of task 7.1, item `hil-rig`, artifacts `hil-rig-self-test.md`, `hil-rig-edges.txt`, `hil-rig-trace.txt`): the boards by serial (the target and the stimulus required; a probe not in the rig is reported); the analyzer seen by sigrok; the stimulus image flashed, read back, hashed, and the board reset; the analyzer capturing the stimulus mirror and the response line while the target image is flashed and run with the console, then stopped (SIGINT; sigrok-cli writes what it has); the target image read back and hashed; the Kernel's trace read and checked for an interrupt storm; and the capture against the console: each response edge matched to the latest stimulus rise before it, the run being the last N response edges where N is the `pulse` count the target reported (earlier edges are the line floating while the target is flashed), every stimulus rise inside the run answered before the next one and within the Task's 15 ms deadline, the stimulus period within 2 % of 18 ms. Unattended: every command is bounded, a locked access port or an absent board is a verdict, not a hang, and nothing waits for an operator. First passing run: 10 pulses answered, latencies 66.5 to 7,211.5 µs with a median of 88 µs (the slow ones are pulses arriving while `sensor` holds `state` at Ceiling 7, answered at the unlock), 515 stimulus pulses seen at 18,000 µs with a 107 µs width.

**Findings on the rig** (each recorded where it belongs):

- **An interrupt storm at the end of the operating duration (Kernel defect, fixed).** With the stimulus at a random phase to the schedule, about one run in eight ended with `control`'s Job at 190 ms missing its deadline. The Kernel's RAM trace showed GPIOTE (vector 22) re-entering the Kernel every 27 µs from 188.6 ms to the end, and nothing below it running again. Cause: an event too late for its Job to complete within the operating duration makes no Release (R9.9), and the intake path left the source unmasked; a level-triggered event that no Job will ever clear then pends again at once, every time, until the operating end, up to one deadline's worth of time (R19.1 broken at the end of the duration). TIMER1 never showed it because its fixed phase never put a pulse in the last 15 ms. `Kernel::intake` now masks the source on that refusal (`Timing::mask`; DD-06); the Verus specification of `intake` states it (`unchanged_but_log_and_mask`), rsk-logic and rsk-kernel verify, the Kani harness `intake_late_event_masks_source` checks the late and the in-time cases, and 16 of 16 runs then passed with no storm in the trace.
- **A Kernel trace in RAM.** The `trace` feature's semihosting halts the core; the new `trace-ram` feature (HIL builds only) keeps the same entry-point trace in a 512-entry ring in Kernel RAM (`TRACE_RING`, tag, value, time-base ticks; `now()` calls excepted, which the Tasks' busy loops would flood it with), placed in `.kernel.noinit`, Kernel RAM the reset path does not zero, so the rig reads it over the probe after the run, through the reset below. The self-test fails on a storm (32 or more re-entries of one vector without a dispatch).
- **The board resets when the probe detaches.** After the semihosting exit, probe-rs's detach leaves the core to re-execute the exit breakpoint with no debugger: a HardFault, then a lockup and a watchdog reset (RESETREAS.LOCKUP and DOG), and the Application boots again with boot check bit 7 failed into the safe state. The first run after flashing on 2026-11-01 found the board in that state. Two changes: the safe state and the exit skip semihosting when no debugger is attached (`hw::debugger_attached`, DHCSR.C_DEBUGEN; the QEMU board reports attached, since its semihosting needs no debugger), so a board left running settles in the idle loop with the watchdog fed; and the trace ring survives the reset. The breakpoint re-execution itself remains (probe-rs's detach behaviour, noted for `hil-watchdog`).
- **A board running without a debugger must never semihost.** Before the guard above, a target left running after a session restarted every 200 ms (fault, watchdog reset, boot, run), which a capture longer than the run showed as hundreds of response edges after it.
- **Windowing the capture on the run.** The response line floats high while the target is flashed and is driven low when its Partition's first Job configures the pin; the stimulus board runs before and after the target. The analysis therefore takes the run as the last N response edges, N being the count the target reports, and reports the rest apart.
- **Probe selection by position is wrong with more than one board.** `cargo xtask hil` ran whichever probe `probe-rs list` showed first; with three attached that is arbitrary. The rig file names every board by serial, and a run without the file requires exactly one probe.

**Open after 7.1.** The stimulus board's spare lines for storms (7.5; hardware PPI from a TIMER to GPIOTE on board B, no CPU); probe-rs's detach behaviour. The trace pins and the release-timing test followed on 2026-11-01 and 02 (section 11.3).

### 11.3 Release timing over PAR-05 (2026-11-01 and 02, task 7.3, R9.10)

**The configuration.** Two declaration variants of the P1 demo for the rig (`nrf52840_timing`, 61 minutes, the PAR-05 run: the longer of one hour and 1,000 hyperperiods of 200 ms; `nrf52840_timing_short`, 3 minutes, the pipeline check), built with the HIL features plus `timing` or `timing-short`: every periodic offset 0, so that all five Periodic_Tasks' nominal instants coincide every 200 ms; `sensor` holding `state` (Ceiling 7) for 12 ms from each of its 20 ms instants, so that a Critical_Section is in progress at `control`'s nominal instants 10 ms later (control is blocked until the unlock, within its deadline); `sensor` at Priority 1, the lowest, so that its injected late Jobs starve nobody; every 1,024th `sensor` Job running 38 ms more (50 ms in all) under the Partition crate's `timing` feature, past two of its nominal instants and short of the third, in Partition 0 (level A, RECORD_AND_CONTINUE, R18.6) with its MIT threshold raised to 1,000; the stimulus board releasing `pulse` every 18 ms throughout. The target's own report judges the run: every count within two Jobs of the operating duration over the period, `sensor` short by exactly its discarded Releases (one per late Job), no fault and no deadline miss other than the late Jobs' own (its Budget overrun, its deadline miss, its held Release, R9.7, and Partition 0's MIT_Violation for the discarded one, R9.8), the FP state converged.

**The record.** A HIL build keeps the Kernel's release-timing record in no-init RAM (`RELEASE_STATS`, feature `trace-ram`): at every time-base interrupt the arch wrapper notes the instant and the cycle counter, calls the verified handler, and for every periodic Release whose due instant moved records the lag `(now - due) * 4 + handler cycles` in CPU cycles per Task (count, maximum, sum, a log2 histogram), counts due instants that are not `O + k*T`, and keeps the last 256 Releases. The lag includes the handler's own work up to the point the Release is pending (the trace entries and the pins of the HIL build included) and the entry cycles of the stub; it is the J_rel the Analyzer assumes, measured on this board. The rig reads the record after the run, with the trace ring and the console, and `cargo xtask hil --release-timing [--short]` writes the report under `docs/hil/`.

**The event mirror.** The 32-entry event log of R14.7 is overwritten many times over in an hour whose late Jobs each log three or four expected events, so the HIL build also mirrors every event Kernel_Arch sees the verified logic push (its sequence number, kind, Task, Partition, instant, detail; the last 512, `EVENT_MIRROR`) and freezes the trace ring at the first event outside the run's expected set, which the system crate declares (`TRACE_EXPECT`: for the release-timing configuration, `sensor`'s overruns, misses, held Releases, and MIT_Violations) and the entry crate passes to the Kernel before boot. Both records are cleared at instant 0 rather than at boot, so that the reboot the board takes when the probe detaches (a failed boot check into the safe state, section 11.2) appends its own events after the run's instead of erasing them; the rig reads the run's events as those up to its safe-state event. A run thus leaves the event that first went wrong and the 512 Kernel entries leading to it.

**Results of the pipeline check (3 minutes, after the fixes below):** 40,487 periodic Releases recorded over the five Periodic_Tasks, every nominal instant `O + k*T`, lag at most 5,090 cycles (79.5 µs; the tick that releases five Tasks at once), control 18,000 of 18,000 Jobs, sensor 8,992 of 9,000 (eight late Jobs, eight discarded Releases), log 4,500, filter 7,200, sampler 1,800, 10,000 of 10,000 stimulus pulses answered within 70 µs to 12.4 ms (median 2.4 ms: the pulse waits behind sensor's 12 ms Critical_Section; never past its 15 ms deadline), the trace without a storm, the dispatch timeline showing the Tasks executing 71 % of the run. **Results of the PAR-05 run (61 minutes, 2026-11-02, `docs/hil/release_timing_nrf52840_timing.md`):** 823,317 periodic Releases over the five Periodic_Tasks (control 365,999, sensor 182,821, log 91,499, filter 146,399, sampler 36,599; the record misses the first of each because the instant-0 Releases precede the arch wrapper's first diff), every nominal instant `O + k*T`, lag at most 6,789 cycles (106.1 µs) with means of 3,248 to 3,735 cycles (51 to 58 µs) and every lag in the 2^12 or 2^13 bucket (64 to 128 µs), 13 counter wraps with the trace's instants ordered across them (R9.5), no storm, the target's counts exact (control 366,000, sensor 182,822 of 183,000 with 178 late Jobs and 178 discarded Releases, log 91,500, filter 146,400, sampler 36,600, echo 121,999), 1,324 events all of the late Jobs' expected kinds (462 overruns, 139 misses, 279 held Releases, 139 MIT_Violations) and the trace never frozen, 203,333 of 203,333 stimulus pulses answered within 70 µs to 12.5 ms (median 2.4 ms, none past the 15 ms deadline), the Tasks executing 71.5 % of the hour.

### 11.4 Fault injection (2026-11-02, task 7.5, R14.11, R18.8, R19.3)

**The response suite (R14.11).** The demo's response configuration (`nrf52840_response`, 2 seconds; features `response` of the three Partition crates) declares one Partition for each response of the closed sets of R14.5 and injects, at fixed instants of the time base (never by Job count, so that a restarted Partition does not repeat them), the fault that triggers each: part0 (level A) RECORD_AND_CONTINUE for `control`'s Budget overrun at 200 ms, RECORD_ONLY for `sensor`'s own deadline miss during its 120 ms Job of 1,305 ms, and SAFE_STATE for `control`'s MemManage fault at 1,900 ms; part1 (level C) END_JOB for `log`'s overrun at 400 ms and RESTART_PARTITION for its fault at 800 ms; part2 (level B) END_JOB for `filter`'s fault at 250 ms, END_JOB escalated to RESTART_PARTITION (R18.4) for its overrun inside its Critical_Section at 500 ms, and STOP_PARTITION for `filter`'s deadline missed while pending behind `sensor`'s 120 ms Job (1,350 ms). The rig (`rsk-hil::response`) passes the run only when the Kernel's event mirror holds exactly the expected events in order and in their windows (the consequences of the 120 ms Job on the delayed Tasks tolerated), the counts show the declared effects (part1's and part2's state back at zero after their restarts, part2's Tasks silent after its stop), and every other Partition ran unchanged (R14.9: part0's counts exact to its own safe state; `pulse` answering before part1's restart, after it, and after part2's stop, seen by the analyzer). The suite is the job's step `hil-response` (Evidence_Item `hil-response`, about a minute) and `cargo xtask hil --response`.

**Results (2026-11-02, `docs/hil/response_nrf52840_response.md`):** pass, 45 events, every response in the expected order: control 190, echo 63, sensor 90 Jobs at the safe state; log 24 and pulse 54 since part1's restart (47 and 101 had they kept their state); filter 32 and sampler 8 between part2's restart and its stop; 44, 24, and 24 pulses answered in the three phases.

**Found by the response suite:**

- **A Partition restart did not re-initialize the Partition** (R14.5). `apply_core` acknowledged the Kernel's re-initialization request and did nothing: a restarted Partition kept its state, its `log` count never returned to 0, so its first Job never restarted the `pulse` source, which is the observation of 11.3. `Action::ReinitPartition` now zeroes the Partition's range of the linker's zero table (every Partition static is zero-initialized by construction: the Generator places Partition `.data` in a no-load section, so a static with another initial value would have been wrong since reset, which the design now states); the Partition Init of PR-34 is still not built.
- **KD-R9.6 (fixed): the running Job's own deadline miss was not reported when a Release of the same Task became due at the same instant.** `timer_event` served the due slots in slot order, and a Task's release slot precedes its deadline slot: the Release that became due at the instant of the running Job's deadline (deadline = period, as the demo declares) re-armed the Task's single deadline slot for its own deadline before the miss was served, so every late Job's own miss was lost and the held Release's (one period later) reported instead. The suite recorded the Kernel as it was (the miss at 1,345 ms) while the item stayed open. Fixed on 2026-11-02 after the hour-long stress run: `timer_event` now serves the due deadline events of an instant before its other events; with the Config_Checker bounding every deadline by the period or MIT, the running Job's deadline is at or before its Task's next Release, so one slot holds every unserved deadline. `intake` likewise serves an overdue deadline of the Task before taking an event in (a deadline event pending behind the intake entry); its contract states the outcome from the state after the miss (`overdue_deadline_served`, `intake_outcome`). The Kani harness `deadline_miss_of_the_running_job_is_reported_at_its_next_nominal_instant` checks the Job's own miss at its next nominal instant and the held Release's at the one after, and the response suite now requires sensor's own miss at 1,325 ms before the held Release's at 1,345 ms.
- **RECORD_AND_CONTINUE recorded one Overrun a dozen times.** The Budget compare of a Job that runs on past its Budget is re-armed with no remainder at every resume after a preemption, and fired at once; each firing logged the Overrun again (12 records of `sensor`'s one 120 ms Job). `budget_event` now records an Overrun once per Job.
- **Preempted busy waits do not reach their Budgets.** An injected overrun written as "busy for 9 ms by the time base" under a 6 ms Budget never overran: the Job's execution, which the Budget measures, was below 6 ms once `sensor`'s Critical_Section and `control` took their share of the 9 ms. R18.1 working as specified; the injections now busy-wait for 20 to 25 ms.

**The stress configuration (R18.8, R19.3).** `nrf52840_stress` (61 minutes, PAR-05) and `nrf52840_stress_short` (3 minutes): the plain demo's declaration with the lower-criticality Partitions misbehaving throughout under the Partition crates' `stress` feature (part1's `log` exceeding its Budget outside a Critical_Section every fourth Job; part2's `filter` holding its Resource for 250 µs, its maximal Critical_Section, every Job and exceeding its Budget inside it every eighth, so that the escalated restart churns part2 every 200 ms; `sampler` exceeding its Budget every second Job) and the stimulus board's `storm` feature toggling the stimulus line as fast as its core can write the port for 16 of every 18 ms (several million edges per second into the target's GPIOTE, the analyzer's mirror quiet). The rig (`cargo xtask hil --stress [--short]`) runs the Analyzer on the configuration's Task_Model with placeholder timing (a copy of the job's Build_Manifest carrying the configuration's own Generated_Config checksum, R28.8) and passes the run only when every Job of part0 (level A) completed within the WCRT the Analyzer computed for its Task (R18.7, R18.8), the stormed source cost at most one intake per MIT window (R19.1: DD-06's masking, whatever the event rate) with the response line toggling once per window, no event of part0's Tasks was recorded (the trace freeze watches them), and the release-timing checks of 11.3 hold.

**Found by the stress configuration:**

- **A killed Job's late-arriving SVC took the Target to the safe state (fixed).** The second short run ended at 36.9 s with a Kernel-attributed panic: the trace freeze left the ring at the event (`docs/hil/stress_short_panic_2026-11-02/`): `log`'s Budget overrun at a tick, END_JOB, the unwind of its level, then an SVC entry numbered 48, which `svc_core` treats as a panic of the executing Job, and with no Job executing, as the Kernel's. 48 is the low byte of 0xBF30, WFI: the SVC handler had decoded the idle activity's return address. The Job had executed `SVC` (its busy wait polls `now()`), the time-base exception arrived during the exception entry and was served first (late arrival, Armv7-M B1.5.11), killed the Job and unwound its level, and the pending SVC then tail-chained onto the resumed idle context. A lower-criticality Partition's overrun thus ended the run, against R18.7. The SVC handler now treats an entry whose frame's return address does not follow an SVC instruction as a stale SVC of a killed Job and ignores it (`arch::cm4::svc`, `SVC_STALE`); the window is a few cycles per SVC and the stress configuration, with its Jobs polling `now()` continuously, hit it within a minute.
- **J_rel under stress.** The release lag reached 8,103 cycles (127 µs) with the lower-criticality Partitions restarting and the source stormed, above the rig's 8,000-cycle bound from 11.3; the bound is now 10,000 cycles and the measured values are recorded with it (task 19.3).

### 11.5 Kernel_Timing_Parameters from the rig (2026-11-02, task 19.3, R13.2, R13.4, R33.1, R33.4, R34.6)

**What the rig records.** After a passed release-timing or stress run, `cargo xtask hil --release-timing|--stress [--short]` writes a `rsk-kernel-timing/1` record set (`docs/hil/kernel_timing_<variant>.json`, the run's own copy next to its report) from the Kernel's release-timing record: **J_rel** (the lag from a periodic nominal instant to the Release becoming effective, over every periodic Release of the run; Table 13-1) and, under the stress configuration, **Δ_irq** (one run of the intake handler for the stormed source, over every intake). Each parameter is a measured record in the sense of R33.1: method `measured`, the runs, the maximum observed value, the margin, the scenario (the configuration and its duration) and the conditions (the HIL build's instrumentation, `trace-ram` and `trace-pins`, whose cost the measurement includes: the record is conservative for a build without it). The bound is the maximum observed value times `1 + m`, rounded up (R34.6), with **m = 25 %** for the Kernel_Timing_Parameters the rig measures (the prototype's margin, ORQ-13: a round figure above the spread the hour-long runs showed between configurations, 6,789 to 8,467 cycles for J_rel; to be revisited with the campaign of task 19.2). Runs of one binary fold into the committed record (the runs add up, the maximum and the bound are the larger); a run of another binary replaces it, since the measurements of a binary no longer built are stale.

**Binding and freshness.** A record is bound to the HIL build that produced it (`binary_hash` over its loadable image, the Target and core revision, the clock, the icache setting). The job builds every measurement configuration (`nrf52840_timing[_short]`, `nrf52840_stress[_short]`) beside its other HIL builds, so the Build_Manifest's `hil_binaries` carry their hashes; a committed record whose hash the manifest lists is fresh, any other is stale and the job names it without using it (R33.4). The Analyzer accepts a Kernel_Timing_Parameter set whose binary is a HIL build of the manifest (the same source, toolchain, clock and cache setting as the Flight_Build, with the rig's instrumentation) and says so in the report; a set of a binary the manifest does not list, or of another clock or cache setting, is rejected under R28.3 (`task_19_3_*` and `r13_4_*` in `crates/rsk-analyzer/tests/rules.rs`). The rig fails a run whose observed maximum exceeds the exported bound of `hil-rig.toml` (`j_rel_cycles` 10,000, `delta_irq_cycles` 2,600; R13.4), and the Analyzer rejects a record whose maximum observed value exceeds its own bound (R13.4).

**How the Analyzer takes them.** `rsk-analyzer --placeholders --timing <record>...` overlays the measured parameters on the nominal placeholders (a measurement replaces a placeholder whatever its size and never lowers a measured bound); the report's `MEASURED` statement carries each parameter's provenance and the `PLACEHOLDER` statement no longer names the measured symbols. The measured records are 64 MHz nRF52840 records and apply to the measurement configurations' Task_Models, not to the QEMU Flight_Build's (25 MHz; the clock check rejects them there): the job's `analysis-rta-p1-demo` item stays the placeholder analysis of the Flight_Build, and one `analysis-rta-<variant>-measured` item per fresh record analyses that configuration with placeholder WCET_Records and the measured parameters. The bench criterion of the stress suite (part0's Jobs within their WCRTs, 11.4) keeps the placeholder analysis.

**Records (2026-11-02, after the KD-R9.6 fix).** `nrf52840_stress_short` (3 minutes): J_rel 10,584 cycles from a maximum of 8,467 cycles (132 µs) over 40,495 Releases; Δ_irq 2,585 cycles from a maximum of 2,068 cycles (32.3 µs) over 11,953 intakes. `nrf52840_timing_short` (3 minutes): J_rel 7,174 cycles from a maximum of 5,739 cycles (89.7 µs) over 40,487 Releases (no storm: the stress configuration's larger lag is the storm's and the restarting Partitions' cost, 11.4). ### 11.7 Isolation: the MPU access matrix and the EasyDMA cases (2026-11-02, task 7.4, R16.8, R17.7)

**The configuration.** The demo's isolation configuration (`nrf52840_isolation`, 3.6 seconds; feature `isolation` of the three Partition crates, which pulls the rsk-kernel feature `hw-dt`) declares every Partition's fault response END_JOB, so that one Task per Partition walks the access matrix one attempt per Job: a refused access ends that Job with a MemManage or BusFault event, and the next Job makes the next attempt. The walkers are `control` (part0), `log` (part1), and `sampler` (part2, after its EasyDMA cases). The matrix is generated, not written: the Generator emits each Partition's memory map into its `rsk_target::layout` (the Kernel's, the shared code's, and every Partition's code and RAM regions, stack sizes, and peripheral windows, DD-07), and `rsk::matrix` enumerates from it, against every other Partition in index order, a read, a write, and a fetch of its code, a read and a write of its data above its stack, a write into its stack, and a read and a write of its first peripheral window (skipped when it owns none), then against the Kernel a read and a write of its RAM, a read and a fetch of its flash, a read and a write of the Private Peripheral Bus (NVIC_ISER0, which a privileged Kernel reads and writes at every dispatch), since 2026-11-05 a read and a write of the first Kernel-owned peripheral after the PPB in the layout's Kernel peripheral intervals (CLOCK on the nRF52840; R16.2, the class the layout proof gained that day), and a write to the word below the Partition's own stack (R16.5): 25 attempts per Partition, 71 with an address (23 and 65 before the Kernel-owned peripheral class). The attempt index advances before the access and a returning access sets its bit in `survived`. The rig (`rsk-hil::isolation`) mirrors the enumeration from the Task_Model and judges: per walker, the fault events of its Task in order against the attempts, each followed by an END_JOB of the Task; the Kernel's new fault record (`FAULT_RING`, the CFSR and MMFAR, BFAR, or stacked PC of every fault) holding one entry per attempt with the attempted address and class (IACCVIOL for a fetch, DACCVIOL with MMARVALID for data, a precise BusFault with BFAR for the PPB); the walkers' counters (every attempt made, none returned); and the trace never frozen. Endpoint storage has no attempt: no Endpoint exists before Phase P4.

**The EasyDMA cases (R17.7).** `sampler`'s first seven Jobs request, on SPIM0's channel 0, its own 16-byte buffer (accepted), a local array in the Partition's stack (refused, R17.1), another Partition's RAM, the Kernel's RAM, 16 bytes starting 8 bytes before the Partition's RAM ends, the buffer on an absent channel (all refused), and the buffer again (accepted); each refusal is a `dma_rejected` event with its END_JOB. The safe state reads SPIM0's registers for the report (the reset the board takes on probe detach clears them): after the run RXD.PTR holds the buffer's address and RXD.MAXCNT 16, the TXD channel is 0, both LIST registers 0, ENABLE 0, no STARTED or END event, RXD.AMOUNT 0: the accepted request's pointer and count and nothing else, no transfer started (R17.2: a refused request reaches no register). For R17.6 `filter`'s Job of 50 ms exceeds its Budget (part2's Overrun_Response RESTART_PARTITION) and the Kernel's new actions record (`ARCH_ACTIONS`: every EasyDMA stop task and every Partition re-initialization, in order) shows SPIM0's stop task triggered before part2's re-initialization. ### 11.8 Hardware_Model differential tests (2026-11-02, task 3.3, R49.3)

**The tests.** The rsk-kernel feature `hw-dt` (HIL builds only) adds two privileged boot phases and four Task-side probes, each recording into `DT_RECORD` (no-init RAM, left alone by the reboot the board takes on probe detach) or the per-Task state, and `rsk-hil::dt` compares every observation with the Hardware_Model's prediction, one row per observation and one verdict per rule (`docs/hil/hw_model_differential_nrf52840.md`, from the isolation suite's run). The early phase runs before `boot_core`, with the NVIC and the MPU at reset, and uses two vectors the Application leaves unbound (IRQ 30 and 31) and two SVC numbers the Kernel does not use (14, 15), which `irq_entry` and `svc` hand to the phase's own handlers while it is active: the implemented priority bits and the DD-02 encoding read back (HW-01); BASEPRI and BASEPRI_MAX (HW-03); both probe exceptions pended under PRIMASK and released by CPSIE I under BASEPRI 0x80, the more urgent taken and the other held until BASEPRI clears (HW-02, HW-03, HW-04); the arbitration order with unequal priorities and with equal ones pended in the opposite order, the lower exception number first (HW-05); IABR and ICSR after the returns (HW-07); Thread mode made unprivileged, a CPSID I there ignored, an SVC from it served privileged, the probe exception's handler reading CONTROL.nPRIV and the SCS and handing the privilege back (HW-04, HW-08); the SVC exception frame with patterns in R0 to R3 and R12, the stacked PC, xPSR.T, and the frame's alignment (HW-06); CYCCNT counting and wrapping (HW-13); and the cycles from one handler's exit to the next's entry with the next already pending against pended after the return (HW-14, tail-chaining). The late phase runs after `boot_core`: FPCCR, FPDSCR, ACTLR, CCR, MPU_CTRL, and PRIGROUP read back (HW-10, HW-11, HW-07, HW-09, HW-01) and the TIMER compare rule on CC[2], a channel the time base does not use, for distances of 0 to 16 ticks (HW-12). The Task-side probes are `filter`'s (an FPU Task): FPSCR as its first Job finds it and, every Job, R2 to R5, R8, R10, R12 and S0 to S31 loaded with patterns, a spin of about 3 ms during which control, echo, sensor, and the Kernel preempt it, and the registers compared (HW-06, HW-10, Property 2); and the isolation suite's matrix (HW-09, including the stack-overflow write) and its PPB attempts' BusFaults, precise with BFAR valid (HW-08, HW-11).

**Results (2026-11-02).** Thirteen rules match as stated: every observation of HW-01 to HW-11, HW-13, and HW-14's tail-chaining (154 cycles from exit to entry against 235 with a return between) is the model's. **HW-12 is a mismatch, and the model was wrong:** compares written 0 to 7 ticks ahead of the counter captured just before the write never fired, 8 ticks and more fired every time (the capture, the addition, and the write take about 7 ticks of the 16 MHz counter); the model's `MIN_COMPARE_DISTANCE` of 2 is refuted. Disposition: the constant is now 16 (twice the smallest distance that fired), the Kernel's `compare_target` (proven equal to `compare_for`) arms no compare closer, and the Kernel's correctness never rested on the constant alone: the nRF driver pends the time-base interrupt when the counter has passed the instant by the time the compare is written (11.1), which is what carried every run before the measurement. ASM-19 is confirmed as a real limit with its value.

**Not tested, with dispositions (R49.3):** the MSR PRIMASK and BASEPRI cases of HW-04 from unprivileged mode (no Task's code may contain them, PR-22, Link_Checker; the CPS case, the one `lemma_job_cannot_set_mask` rests on, is tested); the subregion-disable and overlap rules of HW-09 (no view of this Application sets SRD bits or has two enabled regions over one address; the encoding is Kani-checked at field level); late arrival in HW-14 (a window of a few cycles no probe can hit; observed in the field by the response suite's `SVC_STALE`, 11.4).

### 11.9 Boot checks: mismatched values and corrupted tables (2026-11-04, task 7.2, R12.6)

**The suite.** The HIL build with the rsk-kernel feature `boot-mutation` reads a mutation word before its checks; the rig (`rsk-hil::boot_checks`) writes the word over the probe, flashes and runs the build, and judges the target's report, once per case, then runs the build with the word 0. The word lives at the top of the nRF52840's RAM, above every region of the layout: a no-init word of the Kernel's was overwritten by the flashing algorithm probe-rs loads at the start of RAM (the first run of the suite had every case boot clean). Each mutation makes exactly one check fail: a recorded value differing from the Target's (the CPUID, the implemented priority bits, the clock source, the recorded `CONFIG` and `LAYOUT` checksums, the interrupt-line count, through the trusted table accessors of `SysTables`), the `CONFIG` table corrupted in memory (a Task's Budget in the Kernel's copy, so the computed checksum differs), a register state the check rejects (PRIGROUP changed, the instruction cache enabled, written before the checks), and the watchdog started with a 50 ms reload and never fed (the reset lands inside the run; probe-rs is told not to catch the reset vector, so the next boot reports). A case passes when the report shows the safe state reached, the `boot_check` event with the failed check's bit and no other, every per-Task counter 0 (no Task released), and no event beyond the boot information (the watchdog case also logs the reset). The suite is the job's step `hil-boot-checks` (about two minutes) and `cargo xtask hil --boot-checks`.

**Results (2026-11-04, `docs/hil/boot_checks_nrf52840.md`):** pass, ten of ten cases, bits 8, 4, 10, 9, 11, 5, 5, 13, 6, and 7 each recorded alone, no Task released in any; the unmutated build passes afterwards. Without a case, by nature: the implementer and part-number checks and the MPU region count compare the Target against the Kernel's constants, not a recorded value, and the DWT and the FPU cannot be removed from the silicon; the host test `boot_checks_pass_on_the_expected_board_and_fail_one_bit_per_check` covers those on simulated registers.

**Found while building the suite:** probe-rs's flashing algorithm occupies the start of RAM (a mutation word there is lost); probe-rs halts at the reset vector after a watchdog reset unless told otherwise (`--no-catch-reset`); the demo's 200 ms run ends, and its safe state feeds the watchdog, before the 0.5 s reload elapses, so the watchdog case shortens the reload. None is a Kernel finding. The review of task 3.4 (11.10) changed the reset-reason read the watchdog case relies on (anomaly 136).

### 11.10 Errata review (2026-11-04, task 3.4, R11.6, R49.4)

**Sources.** The Arm Cortex-M4 and Cortex-M4 with FPU Software Developers Errata Notice v3 (ARM-ECM-0426805, 2019: six errata, all present in r0p0 and r0p1, the Target's r0p1 among them) and the nRF52840 Revision 3 errata (build code Fx0; the rig's boards are AAF0), 57 anomalies. The review table is the `hw_model.rs` header (R49.4): for each erratum and for each anomaly that touches a peripheral or behaviour the Kernel uses, its applicability, the workaround applied, and the test that shows the workaround in place.

**Errata of the core.** 838869 (a store buffered across an exception return can vector the next interrupt wrongly) applies: every naked stub ends with `dsb` before its exception return (DD-01), and the Link_Checker now checks it statically on every binary (`ERR-838869`: every `bx` of a naked stub immediately preceded by `dsb`, a `cpsie` between being no store), the test the task asked for; the hour-long runs of 7.3 and 7.5 ran with it. 776924 (VDIV/VSQRT with very short ISRs) requires lazy FP stacking, which the policy disables (FPCCR.LSPEN = 0, the notice's first workaround; `hil-dt-hw-10` reads it back). 752770 (interrupted loads to SP) has no instruction of its class in the Kernel; the Link_Checker now decodes every load to SP as a dynamic SP write, which fails the build for Kernel code (`ERR-752770` note). 839676 (fused MAC on subnormals with FZ clear) is outside the Kernel (no FP arithmetic, R6.6); the declaration's `fp_flush_to_zero = true` (2026-11-04) sets FPDSCR.FZ at boot so that every Job's FP context starts in flush-to-zero mode, the notice's workaround, and `hil-dt-hw-10` reads FPDSCR back as declared and sees `filter`'s first context start from it (the isolation configuration declares it; FPSCR 0x0100_0000 observed). 806422 (ITM with global timestamping) and 1299509 (FPB corruption on an asynchronous reset) concern debug blocks the Kernel never uses.

**Anomalies of the chip.** 136 (RESETREAS bits set beside RESETPIN after a pin reset) applied: the boot read DOG unconditionally and would have entered the safe state after a pin reset; `hw::boot_info` now ignores the other bits when RESETPIN is set and clears every set bit (the host test and the boot-check suite's watchdog case cover it). 155 (a GPIOTE IN event twice on close edges) is masked by the MIT policy and never met on the rig (203,333 of 203,333 edges answered once); 87 (a spurious wake from idle with the FPU) only re-enters the idle loop; 36 (CLOCK registers not reset) is why the boot starts and checks HFCLK explicitly; 78, 213, 242 do not apply (the time base never stops, no System OFF, no flash writes); 176 and 184 are bench procedures probe-rs follows; the SPIM3 anomalies do not reach the demo's SPIM0.

### 11.11 Cycle attribution: the Budget accounting against the DWT (2026-11-04, task 7.5, R18.1, ASM-06)

**The test.** The demo's cycle configuration (`nrf52840_cycles`, 2 seconds; feature `cycles` of part0 and part1) has `control` (Priority 7, the highest: nothing but the Kernel's own entries interrupts it) and `log` (Priority 2, released 4 ms into its 40 ms period so that `sensor`'s 6 ms Critical_Section, `control`, and the Kernel's work for them preempt every one of its Jobs) execute the same 8,000-iteration spin (`rsk::spin`) and nothing else per Job. The Kernel's record of R34.7 now keeps, per Task, the smallest execution and the sum of all beside the largest, and the rig (`rsk-hil::cycles`) judges: both Tasks ran enough Jobs; their mean executions agree within a tolerance; each Task's executions lie in a narrow band; `log`'s largest response time exceeds its largest execution several times over (it was preempted). The suite is the job's step `hil-cycles` and `cargo xtask hil --cycles`.

**Results (2026-11-04, `docs/hil/cycles_nrf52840_cycles.md`):** pass. `control` 200 Jobs, executions 74,637 to 80,991 cycles, mean 76,697 (1.198 ms); `log` 49 Jobs, 80,137 to 87,031, mean 82,195 (1.284 ms); `log`'s largest response 147,390 ticks (9.2 ms, 589,560 cycles) against its largest execution of 87,031 cycles. **The Kernel's entries by kind (task 19.3).** The same record now keeps every Kernel entry by kind (dispatch, timer, intake, each SVC, fault, epilogue, and the entries that restarted or stopped a Partition or applied an Overrun_Response: count, largest, sum of the Rust half's cycles) and, per Task, the entries that interrupted its Jobs. From it the rig emits, with the 25 % margin, the Kernel_Timing_Parameters Δ_dispatch, Δ_timer, Δ_detect, Δ_lock_in, Δ_lock_out, Δ_complete (the Job-end SVC plus the unwind's epilogue), Δ_svc, Δ_dma_write, Δ_hm, Δ_restart, Δ_stop, and Δ_enforce, whichever the run exercised, into every measurement configuration's record (11.5), and the cycle test emits Δ_entry (R13.6, design.md): the preempted Task's excess spread over the entries that interrupted its Jobs. The Analyzer charges Δ_entry once per Job instance, so that every interfering instance carries it (R29.4). The records of the cycle configuration join the measurement configurations' in the job's manifest and measured analyses.

**The floating-point variant (2026-11-06, task 19.3; Δ_fp_save and Δ_fp_restore).** `nrf52840_cycles_fpu` (`cargo xtask hil --cycles --fpu`; the job's `hil-cycles-fpu`) puts `filter`, the FPU Task, in `log`'s place as the preempted spinner (period 40 ms, offset 4 ms, Budget 6 ms): its low-pass step at the start of every Job makes the context active, so every Kernel entry that interrupts it stacks and unstacks the extended frame (DD-04, eager), which the Budget accounting charges to the Job outside the charge points as it charges the stub's cycles. Measured: `filter`'s excess over `control` 1,900 cycles per interrupting entry (136 entries over 49 Jobs; the means 77,150 and 82,424 cycles, 6.84 % apart against the variant's 15 % tolerance) against Δ_entry 1,782 of the plain configuration's run of the same day: 118 cycles of stacking per entry on the first run, and 1,791 against 1,800 on the campaign's run that evening, below the method's resolution (the difference of two per-entry means, whose run-to-run spread is about 100 cycles). The record therefore carries, per parameter, half of the measured difference or of the 100-cycle resolution, whichever is larger (50 cycles observed, 63 with the margin, when the difference is below it; the architecture's extended frame is 17 words each way, well below), with the condition saying which (`docs/hil/kernel_timing_nrf52840_cycles_fpu.json`, which joins the cycle configuration's record in every measured analysis). The last placeholders of Table 13-1 that an entry does not contain are bounded with this; Δ_release and Δ_wake stay inside their entries and Δ_stamp is measured (11.5).

**Measured: the attribution overhead.** `log`'s Jobs are charged 5,498 cycles (7.2 %, 86 µs) more than `control`'s (4,878 on the run before): at every Kernel entry that interrupts a Job, the cycles between the exception entry and the handler's charge point and between the last charge point and the exception return (the stub's pushes and pops, and in a HIL build the trace ring and the trace pins) are the interrupted Job's, some hundreds of cycles per entry with the cache off, about a dozen entries per `log` Job here. The tolerance of the test is 10 % and the excess is reported in every run. For the Analyzer this is a per-preemption cost that lands on the preempted Job's Budget rather than on the preempting work: Table 13-1's switching parameters (Δ_sw, task 19.2) must cover it, and a Budget must leave room for it times the Job's possible preemptions (R29.4; the WCET_Records of 11.6 measured with the HIL instrumentation already include it, R34.7). A first version of the test with `control`'s lock and raise SVCs left in showed `control` 9.7 % above `log`: the SVCs' cost is the calling Job's own, charged as R18.1 requires.

### 11.13 Loop-bound driving, the EasyDMA traffic scenario, and the branch coverage of the measurement inputs (2026-11-06, task 19.2; R17.4, R34.4, R34.5, R34.8, R34.9, R35.3, R35.4, R53.3)

**The EasyDMA traffic scenario (R17.4, R34.5).** part2 owns the Application's only EasyDMA-capable peripheral, SPIM0, so the worst-case concurrent EasyDMA traffic of all Partitions is SPIM0 at its highest rate throughout: `sampler`'s first Job of each incarnation selects the pins (P0.14 and P0.15, the DK's LED2 and LED3, for SCK and MOSI; P0.16, LED4, for MISO; part1, the owner of `P0`, sets their directions in its first `log` Job, since the SPIM does not and does not clock without SCK connected), sets 8 Mbps and the END_START shortcut, enables the peripheral, requests a 64-byte transfer out of `tx` and one into `rx` (`rsk::dma_request`, the buffers checked, R17.1), and starts it; the shortcut chains the transfers for the whole run, two AHB masters on the RAM at a byte per microsecond each way. Every later Job counts whether a transfer ended since the last (`dma_end`) and clears the event. The rig's step "EasyDMA traffic (R17.4)" judges the counters and SPIM0's registers at the end, and every record of the run names the scenario with what was observed, beside the floating-point scenario (triggered by `filter`'s Jobs, not by the others') and the preemption scenario (every Task at its rate, the stormed source under stress), as R33.1 and R34.5 require. Observed: timing-short, a transfer ended before 1,799 of 1,800 `sampler` Jobs, END set at the end; stress-short, the restarts of part2 every 200 ms re-initialize the counters and the traffic restarts with each incarnation's first Job (END and STOPPED set at the end, SHORTS cleared by the Kernel's stop).

**What the scenario needed and found.** (1) No Partition could start an EasyDMA transfer: its view maps the peripheral read-only (DD-08) and the only service programmed a transfer's pointer and count. The Kernel gained the register-write service DD-08 anticipated, `rsk::dma_write(channel, offset, value)` (SVC 6 with bit 31 of r0 set; `dma_write_core`, Verus-proven with the request's frame conditions; `Action::DmaWrite` in the Hardware_Model and the Kani harness), which writes any register of the Partition's own EasyDMA peripheral except the pointer, count, and list-mode registers of every channel of the window, and refuses the rest as a spatial violation (R17.2); Δ_dma_write covers both services. (2) **A Partition's END_START shortcut survived the Kernel's stop task (R17.6).** The first coverage run of the stress configuration found one restart of about ninety after which a transfer started again within 150 µs of the Kernel's stop task (the first Job of the new incarnation clears STARTED, waits, and reads it set again: `dma_after_stop`): the stop task ends the transfer with an END event, and the shortcut the Partition had set starts the next. The Kernel's stop sequence now clears the peripheral's SHORTS register before its stop tasks (`DmaCfg::shorts` from the Target table's per-class offset, `Action::DmaShortsOff`, Kani checked; Generated_Config regenerated), and the stress runs since then show SHORTS 0 and STOPPED set at the end with no transfer after a stop. The isolation suite (11.7) had not seen this because its Partition never set a shortcut. (3) The demo's safe state counted one accepted request per run under the scenario (`dma_ok`), its criterion `dma_ok + 2 >= sampler Jobs` failing the run; `traffic_step` now counts a confirmed transfer as the Job's accepted request.

**Observations of the first runs with the scenario (open).** (The UsageFault of 2026-11-05 recurred in the evening campaign of 2026-11-06 with its diagnostics, section 13.) The release-timing step's wire latency reached 15,160 µs once in 10,000 against `pulse`'s 15 ms deadline in one timing-short run (12,420 to 12,560 µs in the others), with no deadline miss logged by the Kernel: the Kernel's deadline runs from its timestamp of the event (R9.6), the wire's from the edge, and the difference is the stamp lag (an entry's masking, L_kernel, then the intake handler's timestamp, within Δ_irq; the dispositions of 11.5). The loopback step now lists what executed inside every late window from the trace pins, exports L_kernel (`l_kernel_cycles`, 7,000), and allows an answer later than the deadline by at most the stamp lag when the Kernel logged no miss (L_kernel plus Δ_irq until Δ_stamp was measured the same day, 11.5: 48.6 and 76.7 µs observed, 8,000 cycles exported, which the 160 µs of that run exceeds: a failure by that rule, not reproduced in the runs since). In the same run the agent's largest `control` Job exceeded the trace pin's longest run by 1,418 cycles (7.9 %; 204 cycles, 1.1 %, on the next run; 79 before the scenario) with 18,322 runs for 18,000 Jobs on the pins: the check's tolerance is now a tenth beside the two samples of quantization, and the run-count excess (a Job's run split by a Kernel entry that drives the pins, or a short run between Jobs) is to be read from the capture. Both stay listed in section 13.

**The 160 µs late answer, an attempt at the explanation (2026-11-07).** What the figure cannot be: a pairing artefact of the analyzer (each response edge answers the latest rise at or before it, so a dropped rise lengthens a latency by the 18 ms period and an extra rise shortens one; that run had 10,000 response edges for 9,999 answers, so no spurious edge either); a Kernel-side miss missed (KD-R9.6 serves an overdue deadline at the ending Job's observed instant, so a Job that answers before its end and ends before the deadline by the Kernel's clock is never late by more than the stamp lag plus the capture's two samples of quantization, 20 µs). So the answer was within 20 µs of the deadline by the Kernel's clock (its own latency about 15.0 ms, far above the 12.4 to 12.9 ms the other maxima show) and the stamp lagged the edge by about 160 µs. The single-entry basis of L_kernel does not reach that: the longest Rust half of one entry kind is 5,469 cycles (85 µs, the timer's) and Δ_irq 2,088 (33 µs), 118 µs with the capture's quantization 138; yet the hour-long run of 2026-11-07 already observed a stamp lag of 102 µs. The candidate is a masked span longer than any one entry: a timer entry that serves a deadline miss or Overrun of `sensor` (the configuration injects 8 late Jobs and records 16 misses and 8 Overruns a run) ends the Job and unwinds its level inside the same PRIMASK span, so the span is the timer's Rust half plus the epilogue's, 5,469 + 4,259 = 9,728 cycles (152 µs), which with Δ_irq gives 185 µs, above the 160 observed; a pulse edge lands in one of those 16 to 24 spans about once in eight 3-minute runs, and the coincidence with an answer at the deadline makes the observation the rarity it was. The consequence, if so, is not the one late wire answer but L_kernel itself: the record's longest single Rust half understates the longest masked span whenever an entry's response unwinds a Job, and the Analyzer's blocking term takes L_kernel. The test: the HIL build measures every masked span end to end (DWT at the stub's `cpsid i` and `cpsie i`), records its maximum, and the record carries L_kernel from that span rather than from the longest Rust half; the timing configuration should then show about 150 µs and the next late answer, if any, its own span from the loopback step's listing. Listed in section 13 against L_kernel's basis until measured.

**Measured the same day: the span is longer than the sum.** The HIL build now records the masked span end to end (`ARCH_SPAN`): a span opens at the first Rust half of a Kernel entry and closes at the Rust half whose return reaches a context in which a Kernel-level interrupt is taken, which the close decides from ICSR.VECTPENDING (a pending Kernel-level exception, or a dispatch vector the resumed BASEPRI admits, tail-chains into the next handler under PRIMASK and keeps the span open; an unwind keeps it open through its epilogues; a pending vector BASEPRI masks, or nothing pending, closes it, since the Job then resumes and an intake is taken). The rig's step "masked span (L_kernel)" reports the maximum, and the record's L_kernel is the maximum plus Δ_entry when the span record exists (the longest single Rust half otherwise). The first timing-short run on it: **18,748 cycles (293 µs) over 11.7 million spans, closed by a timer entry**, against the longest single Rust half of 5,469: a timer entry serving a release instant of the injected late Jobs, whose response ends the Job, unwinds its level, and tail-chains into the dispatch of the next one, all under one mask. The 160 µs answer lies inside it and is explained: the one-off was a `pulse` edge inside such a span with the Job's own answer at its deadline. The campaign of the same day measured every configuration: 205 to 310 µs in the short runs, 340.8 µs in the hour-long timing run (238 million spans), 408.2 µs in the hour-long stress run (144 million spans, closed by a dispatch entry: the storm's intakes chain into the dispatches they release), and 1.8 ms in the Kernel coverage build, whose instrumented Kernel is no timing evidence (section 14). The export `l_kernel_cycles` is 35,000 (the stress run's span plus Δ_entry with the margin), and the Analyzer's blocking term takes each record's own value from this refresh on.

**Branch coverage of the measurement inputs (R34.4, R53.3).** `cargo xtask hil --coverage [--stress]` builds the short configuration as the coverage build: the three Partition crates compiled with `-C instrument-coverage -Z coverage-options=branch -Z no-profiler-runtime` by the coverage toolchain (`toolchain/decision.toml [coverage]`: nightly-2026-09-17, rustc 1.100.0-nightly, LLVM 23.1.1, since the pinned 1.95.0 has neither the branch option nor a way to omit the profiler runtime; every crate keeps the legacy symbol mangling the Link_Checker reads), through a `RUSTC_WRAPPER` the command writes; the Kernel, the API, and the declaration as in every HIL build. The counters lie in `.p<i>.cov` of the generated linker script, in each Partition's RAM above its image and outside its range of the zero table (a restart re-initializes the image and keeps the counts), zeroed at instant 0 by the Kernel's `coverage` feature (`__rsk_cov_table`; the reboot the probe's detach causes never reaches instant 0, so the counts survive it as the records do); rustc lowers every counter update to an atomic 64-bit add the Cortex-M4 has no instruction for, so the Kernel provides the libcall `__atomic_fetch_add_8` in the shared code region (not atomic: a lost increment changes a count, never whether a branch executed). The profile data and names stay in flash under their own section names (llvm-cov looks `__llvm_prf_names` up by name), the coverage mapping outside the image.
